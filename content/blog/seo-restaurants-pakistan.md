## H1: SEO for Restaurants in Pakistan

For most restaurants, "SEO" means the wrong thing. Website rankings are a secondary concern. The decision happens in the map pack — three results, chosen by someone standing nearby, hungry, deciding in under a minute.

Win those three slots in your area and the website matters far less than anyone selling you a website will suggest.

## Google Business Profile: The Priority List

**Category.** Get specific. "Pakistani Restaurant", "Fast Food Restaurant", "Cafe", "Bakery", "Barbecue Restaurant" — matched to what actually ranks for your queries, not to how you describe yourself.

**Photos.** Restaurants are chosen visually. Food, interior, exterior, seating. Fresh photos monthly matter more than a large one-time upload. This is the highest-return ongoing task and the most neglected.

**Menu.** Add it to the profile with items and prices. Many customers never leave Maps.

**Attributes.** Dine-in, takeaway, delivery, outdoor seating, family seating, parking, card payment, wheelchair access. These feed filtered searches and are frequently left blank.

**Hours, accurately.** Including special hours for Ramadan, which transforms restaurant timings entirely. A customer who arrives at a closed restaurant leaves a one-star review.

**Pin accuracy.** Especially in food streets and plazas where several restaurants share an address. A wrong pin misdirects both customers and delivery riders.

## Reviews Are the Ranking and Conversion Signal

For restaurants, reviews do double duty — they influence whether you appear and whether you are chosen.

**What works:** asking at the table when someone is visibly happy, a QR code on the receipt, a WhatsApp follow-up for delivery orders, and replying to every review including the negative ones.

**What to avoid:** buying reviews, offering discounts for reviews, and staff reviewing from personal accounts. These get filtered and put the listing at risk.

**Responding to negatives** matters more in food than most sectors, because complaints are public, specific and often about a single bad night. A calm, non-defensive reply offering to make it right reads well to everyone who sees it afterwards.

## Website Pages That Actually Earn Traffic

**Menu page in HTML text.** Not a PDF, not an image. A menu locked in a JPEG is invisible to search and unusable on a slow connection. This is the most common restaurant website mistake in Pakistan.

**Location page per branch**, with crawlable address, embedded map, hours and `LocalBusiness` schema.

**Catering and events page.** High-value enquiries with genuine search demand — "catering services in Lahore", "wedding catering rates" — and almost no restaurant competes for them properly.

**Reservation page**, if you take bookings.

**Occasional food content** that matches real searches: what a dish is, how your version differs, regional variations. This works for distinctive cuisines and is wasted effort for generic fast food.

## Reducing Delivery Platform Dependence

Platforms deliver volume and take commission, own the customer, and set the discounts. Search visibility is one of the few levers that shifts orders back to channels you control.

**What helps:** ranking in the map pack so people find you directly, a WhatsApp ordering number on your profile and receipts, direct-order incentives, and building a customer list from orders you take yourself.

This is gradual work, not a switch. But every direct order is commission-free and gives you a customer number you keep.

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**Ramadan transforms the search year.** Iftar and sehri searches spike enormously, timings change completely, and deal-based queries dominate. Update special hours, publish iftar deals early, and prepare content before the month starts. It is the single largest predictable opportunity in the restaurant calendar.

**Food streets and clustering.** In areas where dozens of restaurants share a street, pin accuracy and photo quality do most of the differentiating.

**Family seating is a genuine search filter** in much of Pakistan. Set the attribute and mention it.

**Dish names have multiple spellings.** Biryani, biriyani, biryani; karahi, karhai; nihari, nehari. Reflect real variants naturally in menu text rather than picking one.

**Wedding and event catering** is seasonal and high-value, and most restaurants treat it as an afterthought despite real search volume.

**Delivery area searches** — customers search whether you deliver to their area. Answering this on the site reduces WhatsApp load.

## FAQ

**Do restaurants need a website?**
Yes, but it is secondary to the Google Business Profile. Its main jobs are a readable menu, location pages, catering and reservations.

**Why is my restaurant not showing in Maps?**
Common causes are the wrong primary category, an inaccurate pin, an incomplete profile, or too few genuine reviews relative to competitors nearby.

**Should the menu be a PDF?**
No. A PDF or image menu is invisible to search and difficult to use on a slow connection. Use HTML text.

**How many reviews do we need?**
Compare against restaurants currently holding the pack in your area. There is no fixed number.

**Can SEO reduce our delivery platform commission?**
Indirectly. Better map visibility and direct ordering channels shift some orders to routes you control, gradually.

**How do we prepare for Ramadan?**
Update special hours, publish iftar and sehri offers early, and prepare content before the month starts rather than during it.

**Should we blog about food?**
Only if your cuisine has genuine search interest. For most fast food, profile and photo work returns far more.
