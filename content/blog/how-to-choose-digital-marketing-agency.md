## H1: How to Choose a Digital Marketing Agency

Most businesses choose a marketing agency the same way: three proposals, a comparison of monthly fees, and a decision based on whoever sounded most confident in the meeting.

That process selects for sales skill, not delivery skill. The agency with the best pitch deck is not necessarily the one that will still be paying attention to your account in month four.

This guide is a structured alternative — what to decide before you speak to anyone, what to ask, which answers should concern you, and how to limit the cost of choosing wrong.

---

## Step 1: Decide What You Are Actually Buying

Before contacting a single agency, write down three things.

**The business outcome.** Not "more followers" or "better SEO". Enquiries, bookings, orders, admissions, qualified B2B leads. If you cannot name the outcome, no agency can deliver it and every agency can claim they did.

**Your current numbers.** How many enquiries a month now, from which sources, and roughly what they convert to. Even rough figures give you a baseline to measure against.

**Your constraint.** Budget, time, internal capacity, or a specific channel you know is broken. An agency solving the wrong constraint produces activity without results.

Agencies that receive this brief give better proposals. Agencies that do not ask for it are telling you something.

---

## Step 2: Match the Agency Type to the Problem

"Digital marketing agency" covers very different businesses.

| Agency type | Suited to | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Full-service agency | Businesses needing several channels coordinated | Junior staff doing the actual work |
| Channel specialist (SEO, paid ads, social) | A clearly identified channel problem | Recommending their channel for everything |
| Performance / lead generation agency | Businesses with clear conversion tracking | Lead quality traded for lead volume |
| Creative / content studio | Brand, video, design output | Little accountability for commercial results |
| Freelancer | Small, well-defined tasks | Single point of failure, limited capacity |

A clinic that needs Google Maps visibility does not need a creative studio. A B2B exporter does not need an Instagram content calendar. Matching type to problem removes half the shortlist before any meeting.

## Step 3: The Questions That Separate Agencies

Ask every shortlisted agency the same questions, and compare the answers side by side.

**"Who will actually work on our account, and can we meet them?"**
The person pitching is often not the person doing the work. You want names and roles.

**"What will you do in the first 30 days?"**
A credible answer involves auditing, tracking setup and a prioritised plan. A vague answer involves "getting started on content".

**"How will we measure success, and what will you report?"**
Look for business metrics — enquiries, cost per lead, conversion — not only impressions and reach.

**"What would make you tell us this is not working?"**
Agencies that cannot answer this rarely admit failure when it happens.

**"Who owns the ad accounts, website, analytics and content?"**
The answer should be you. Always.

**"Can we speak to a current client?"**
Not a testimonial on a slide — a conversation.

**"What will you not do for us?"**
An agency that does everything for every budget is spreading itself thin.

---

## Step 4: Answers That Should Worry You

- **Guaranteed rankings or guaranteed lead numbers.** Nobody controls Google's algorithm or your market. Guarantees usually mean the metric has been chosen to be easy.
- **"We are the number one agency in Pakistan."** Unverifiable, and a claim made by a great many agencies at once.
- **Ad accounts created under the agency's ownership.** If you leave, your history, audiences and pixel data leave with them.
- **Reporting that is only reach, likes and impressions.** Activity metrics hide commercial results.
- **Long lock-in contracts before any delivery.** Reasonable minimum terms exist — SEO genuinely takes months — but a year upfront with no exit clause is a sales tactic.
- **No questions about your business.** An agency that proposes without understanding your margins, customers and sales process is selling a template.
- **Very low prices with very broad scope.** Something has to give, and it is usually attention.

## Step 5: Read the Proposal Properly

A proposal should tell you what you will receive, not just what the agency is capable of.

**Check for:**
- Specific deliverables per month, not "ongoing optimisation"
- Who does what — your team versus theirs
- What is included in the fee, and what costs extra (ad spend, content production, tools)
- The reporting schedule and format
- Notice period and handover terms
- What happens to assets and accounts if the engagement ends

**Separate ad spend from management fees.** A proposal that blends them makes it impossible to judge value. Our guide to [digital marketing cost in Pakistan](/blog/digital-marketing-cost-pakistan) breaks down how pricing is typically structured.

## Step 6: Structure a Low-Risk Start

You do not have to commit to a full retainer to find out whether an agency is good.

**Paid audit or strategy phase.** A defined piece of work — an audit, a tracking setup, a channel plan — shows how the agency thinks and communicates before a long engagement.

**Defined trial period.** Two or three months on one channel, with agreed success measures written down in advance.

**Tracking before spend.** Insist that conversion tracking works before significant ad budget goes live. Without it, neither of you will know what worked.

**Agree review points.** A scheduled review at the end of the trial, against the numbers you agreed at the start.

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**WhatsApp is the conversion channel.** Most Pakistani enquiries end in a WhatsApp conversation. An agency that generates leads but has no view on how quickly they are answered is optimising half the funnel.

**City and language matter.** A plan built for Karachi consumers may not suit a Faisalabad manufacturer or a Sahiwal clinic. Ask how the agency has adapted work to markets like yours.

**Payment and invoicing.** Clarify how ad spend is paid — directly on your card, or through the agency — and how currency and tax are handled on platform fees.

## Conclusion

Choosing an agency well is mostly about preparation: know the outcome you want, match the agency type to the problem, ask questions that reveal how they work, and structure the start so a wrong choice is cheap.

The agency worth hiring is usually the one that asked you the most questions — not the one that made the most promises.

## FAQ

**How many agencies should we speak to?**
Three to five is enough. Beyond that, comparison becomes harder, not easier.

**Should we choose the cheapest proposal?**
Not on price alone. Compare deliverables, who does the work, and what is excluded. A cheap retainer with vague scope often costs more in wasted ad spend.

**What minimum contract length is reasonable?**
It depends on the channel. Paid ads can be judged in two to three months; SEO usually needs longer. Be wary of long lock-ins with no exit terms.

**Who should own our ad accounts?**
You should. The agency should be given access, not ownership.

**Is it a red flag if an agency guarantees results?**
Yes, when the guarantee is about rankings or lead numbers they cannot control.

**Should we hire a specialist or a full-service agency?**
A specialist if you know which channel is the problem; full-service if several channels need coordinating and you lack the internal capacity to do it.

**What should an agency report every month?**
Business outcomes — enquiries, cost per lead, conversions — alongside what was done and what changes next.
