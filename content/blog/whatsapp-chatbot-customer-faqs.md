## H1: WhatsApp Chatbot for Customer FAQs

In almost every Pakistani business, a small number of questions account for most inbound message volume. Price, location, timings, delivery, availability, warranty, payment method. The same seven, forever, answered by staff who could be doing something else.

Automating them is the most straightforward win in customer messaging — and the one most likely to quietly go wrong, because an FAQ bot that gives an outdated answer is worse than no bot at all.

---

## Step One: Find Your Actual Questions

Not the ones on your website's FAQ page. The ones customers genuinely send.

Export two weeks of messages, read them, and count. The distribution is nearly always steeper than owners expect — a handful of questions covering the large majority of volume, and a long tail of one-offs.

**Write down the actual phrasing.** People do not ask "what are your operating hours?" They ask "abhi khule ho?" Your bot must recognise the second.

---

## Step Two: Retrieve, Never Generate

This is the single most important design decision, and the difference between a bot you can trust and one that creates disputes.

**Retrieval:** the system looks up your actual price from your actual data and returns it. The model decides *which* fact is relevant; it does not compose the fact.

**Generation:** the model produces a sentence that sounds like an answer. Sometimes it is correct. Sometimes it invents a plausible price you do not charge.

For anything factual — prices, hours, policies, delivery charges, warranty terms — retrieval is the only safe approach. In a price-sensitive market where customers remember quoted figures, a hallucinated number becomes a dispute you will lose.

---

## Step Three: Make It Editable by Your Team

Prices change. Hours change during Ramadan. Delivery charges change with fuel costs. Policies get revised.

If updating an answer requires a developer, the bot becomes wrong within weeks and stays wrong until someone complains. This is the most common way FAQ automation fails — not at launch, but three months later.

**Non-negotiable:** whoever knows the correct answer must be able to change it themselves, in minutes, without a ticket.

---

## Step Four: Design the Escalation

An FAQ bot's job is to answer the known and hand over the rest cleanly.

**Escalate when:**
- The question is not in the knowledge base
- The customer asks the same thing twice, indicating the answer did not land
- Any complaint or frustration is detected
- The customer explicitly asks for a person
- The question touches anything requiring judgment or an exception

**Never loop.** A customer cycling through the same menu three times is a customer about to leave. Two failed attempts should trigger a handover, not a third attempt.

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**Roman Urdu is how questions arrive.** "Kitne ka hai", "kahan ho aap", "delivery free hai?" — the system must recognise these, not just their English equivalents. Test against your real message history.

**Voice notes** are a substantial share of enquiries in many sectors. Decide whether to transcribe or route to a person; ignoring them discards genuine customers.

**Price is the most asked question by a wide margin**, and the one businesses most often deflect. Answering it instantly outperforms forcing a conversation — customers who cannot get a price simply ask a competitor who gives one.

**Special hours matter more than they seem.** A customer told you are open who arrives at a closed shop leaves a one-star review, and young profiles are disproportionately damaged by them.

**Delivery charges by city** are a frequent question for national sellers. "Delivery to Multan kitni hai" needs a real answer, not a generic figure.

**Trust and disclosure.** Say plainly that this is an automated assistant and a person is available. Customers accept it; they resent discovering concealment.

## Keeping It Accurate Over Time

The failure mode is drift, not launch. Build the maintenance in:

- **A named owner** for the knowledge base
- **A monthly review** of answers against current reality
- **An update before predictable changes** — Ramadan hours, seasonal pricing
- **Conversation sampling** to catch questions the bot answered badly
- **A log of escalated questions**, which shows what should be added

That last one is the most useful ongoing signal. Questions repeatedly escalating are questions the knowledge base should cover.

---

## What Success Looks Like

- Share of enquiries resolved without a person
- Median first-response time
- Escalation rate, and whether escalations were appropriate
- Repeat questions from the same customer, indicating unclear answers
- Staff time recovered

The third and fourth need manual sampling. A bot reporting a high resolution rate while customers quietly give up looks identical to one that is working.

## FAQ

**Which questions should I automate first?**
Whichever account for the most volume in your real message history — usually price, location, hours and delivery.

**Can the bot invent a wrong price?**
Only if answers are generated rather than retrieved. Facts should come from your data verbatim.

**How do we keep answers current?**
Give your team direct edit access and assign a named owner. Answers that require a developer to change go stale.

**Does it need to understand Roman Urdu?**
Yes. That is how most questions arrive in Pakistan. Test on your own messages.

**What happens when it cannot answer?**
It should hand over to a person after at most two failed attempts. Looping loses customers.

**Should we publish prices in the bot?**
Generally yes. Customers who cannot get a price ask a competitor who gives one.

**How do we know it is working properly?**
Sample real conversations. A high resolution rate can mean customers gave up rather than got answers.
