## H1: Social Media Marketing for E-commerce

For most Pakistani online stores, social media is not a branding channel. It is the storefront. A large share of orders begin on Instagram or Facebook, move to WhatsApp, and complete on delivery in cash.

That path has specific economics, and understanding them is the difference between a store that looks profitable on a dashboard and one that actually is.

---

## The Real Funnel in Pakistan

1. Someone sees a product in a Reel, a story, or a feed post
2. They comment "price?" or send a DM
3. The conversation moves to WhatsApp
4. Order details, address and contact are collected in chat
5. Order confirmed and dispatched
6. Cash collected at the door — or the parcel is refused

Steps two through four are where most stores lose sales, and step six is where they lose margin.

## Setting Up to Sell

**Product catalogue.** Connect it to Meta so products are taggable in posts and stories and usable in dynamic ads. Most Pakistani stores skip this and lose the retargeting that follows.

**Shop setup.** Even where checkout happens in chat, a browsable shop keeps people from leaving to ask basic questions.

**Pixel and Conversions API.** Without server-side signal, optimisation runs on incomplete data, and in a market where much of the journey happens off-platform in WhatsApp, that gap is large.

**WhatsApp click-to-chat** on the profile, in ads, and in bio links. This is the actual checkout for many stores.

**Prices visible.** In captions, on images, in the catalogue. Withholding price to force a DM generates comment volume nobody can answer and irritates buyers.

## Creative That Sells

**Video over static.** Reels and short video drive reach. A product shown being used, worn or unboxed outperforms a flat product shot consistently.

**Real footage.** Your actual product, in real light, on a real person or in a real setting. Over-polished studio imagery in Pakistan reads as either a large brand or a scam, and buyers are wary of the second.

**Sound-off design.** Captions burned in. Many viewers watch without audio.

**Price and availability stated.** Filters out the unqualified before they consume your response capacity.

**Delivery information.** Whether you ship nationwide, charges, and timeframe. These are the three most common DM questions.

**Social proof.** Genuine customer photos and reviews, used with permission. Never fabricated.

## Comments and DMs Are the Sales Floor

This is where Pakistani e-commerce is won and lost.

A product post generating two hundred comments asking for price is not success if nobody answers them. Those are queued customers, publicly visible, walking away — and every unanswered "price?" tells the next reader that you do not respond.

**What is required:**
- Someone answering comments within minutes during active campaign hours
- A path from comment to DM to WhatsApp that does not lose the thread
- Automated first response outside working hours, when a lot of browsing happens
- Saved replies for the questions that repeat

For stores running ads at volume, manual handling stops being possible. This is the clearest automation case in Pakistani retail.

## The Number That Matters: COD-Adjusted ROAS

Platform-reported ROAS counts orders placed. In a cash-on-delivery market, an order is not revenue until the parcel is accepted.

Between order and acceptance you lose: refused deliveries, uncontactable customers, wrong addresses, and buyers who changed their mind. Each costs you the courier charge in both directions plus handling.

**Calculate your real number:**

1. Take platform-reported revenue
2. Subtract the value of refused and returned orders
3. Subtract courier charges on those failures
4. Divide by ad spend

Stores that only watch platform ROAS scale campaigns that are losing money on delivery economics. This calculation is the single most valuable thing in this article.

**Reducing the gap:** automated order confirmation before dispatch, address verification in chat, prepayment incentives, and clear product information that prevents mismatched expectations.

## Pakistan-Specific Realities

**Instagram skews urban and younger; Facebook reaches wider.** A store selling to smaller cities will reach more buyers on Facebook, whatever the aesthetic appeal of Instagram.

**TikTok offers organic reach** without spend for the right categories and content style — genuinely valuable for stores that can produce in that format.

**Creator collaboration frequently beats paid reach** for local brands. Micro-creators in specific cities and niches, measured properly with unique codes or links.

**Ramadan and Eid dominate the retail calendar**, followed by wedding season. Plan inventory, creative and budget around them rather than spreading spend evenly.

**Roman Urdu performs** in captions and replies for many categories. Test rather than assume.

**Trust signals matter disproportionately.** Visible contact details, a real address, clear return policy and responsive replies affect conversion more than creative polish, because buyers are cautious about advance payment and unknown sellers.

## Retargeting Is Where Margin Lives

Most Pakistani stores run only prospecting campaigns and wonder why costs rise.

**Worth building:**
- Video viewers who watched a meaningful portion
- Profile and post engagers
- Catalogue viewers who did not order
- Past customers, for repeat purchase
- Abandoned WhatsApp conversations

The last one is specific to this market and almost nobody does it. People who started an order conversation and stopped are the warmest audience you have.

## FAQ

**Which platform is best for e-commerce in Pakistan?**
Instagram for visual categories in urban centres, Facebook for wider reach including smaller cities, TikTok for organic reach in the right categories. Most stores need two.

**Should I put prices in my posts?**
Yes. Hiding price generates comment volume you cannot answer and frustrates buyers who will find a seller who tells them.

**Why is my ROAS good but profit poor?**
Almost always cash-on-delivery returns. Platform ROAS counts orders placed, not parcels accepted.

**How do I reduce COD returns?**
Automated order confirmation before dispatch, address verification, prepayment incentives, and accurate product information.

**Do I need a website if I sell on social?**
It helps for trust, catalogue depth and search visibility, but many Pakistani stores start profitably on social plus WhatsApp.

**Are influencers worth it?**
Micro-creators often outperform paid reach for local brands. Measure with unique codes or links rather than impressions.

**What about retargeting?**
Essential, and underused. Abandoned WhatsApp conversations are the warmest audience most Pakistani stores never target.
