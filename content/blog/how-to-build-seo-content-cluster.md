## H1: How to Build an SEO Content Cluster

A content cluster is a group of interlinked pages covering one topic in depth: a central pillar page that addresses the topic broadly, and supporting spoke pages that each cover a specific subtopic in detail.

Clusters work because they match how search engines evaluate expertise. One isolated article on a subject says little. A well-organised set of pages that answers the questions around a subject signals depth — and helps both users and crawlers find related information.

Most explanations of clusters stop at the diagram. This guide is the working process.

---

## Step 1: Choose a Core Topic Tied to Revenue

The best clusters sit close to what you sell.

**A good core topic:**
- Relates directly to a product or service you offer
- Has enough subtopics to support multiple pages
- Has search demand across those subtopics
- Is an area where you have genuine expertise
- Is not so broad it cannot be covered well

**Examples:**
- Too broad: "marketing"
- Good: "WhatsApp automation for businesses"
- Too narrow for a cluster: "WhatsApp greeting message examples" (better as a single spoke)

Start with one cluster. Finish it before starting another.

---

## Step 2: Collect Subtopics and Questions

Gather everything people want to know about the core topic.

**Sources:**
- Keyword research tools — terms containing or related to the topic
- "People also ask" and related searches
- Competitor pages that rank for the topic
- Customer questions from sales calls, WhatsApp chats, emails and support tickets
- Your team's expertise — what beginners misunderstand, what experts debate

Put everything into a single spreadsheet: query, estimated demand, notes on intent.

Customer questions are often the most valuable source, because they surface subtopics competitors miss.

---

## Step 3: Group by Intent, Not by Wording

This is where most clusters go wrong. Different keywords that share the same search intent belong on the same page.

**How to group:**
- Search each query and compare the top results
- If the same pages rank for two queries, they likely share intent — combine them
- If results differ significantly, they need separate pages

**Example groupings for a WhatsApp automation cluster:**
- "WhatsApp chatbot price", "WhatsApp chatbot cost", "how much does a WhatsApp bot cost" → **one pricing page**
- "WhatsApp API vs Business app", "difference between WhatsApp Business and API" → **one comparison page**
- "WhatsApp chatbot for clinics", "WhatsApp appointment booking bot" → **possibly separate**, depending on results

Grouping by intent prevents keyword cannibalisation — multiple pages competing for the same query. See [semantic SEO](/blog/semantic-seo-guide).

---

## Step 4: Design the Pillar Page

The pillar page covers the core topic broadly and serves as the hub.

**A strong pillar page:**
- Targets the main topic query
- Explains the topic comprehensively at an overview level
- Includes a section for each major subtopic, with a summary and a link to the detailed spoke
- Answers the most common questions directly
- Links to relevant service or product pages
- Is kept updated as the cluster grows

**Avoid:** trying to make the pillar page cover every subtopic in full depth. It should summarise and route, not duplicate the spokes.

---

## Step 5: Define Spoke Pages

Each spoke covers one subtopic group in depth.

**For each spoke, define:**
- Primary query and intent
- Page type — guide, comparison, pricing, use case, how-to, industry page
- Key questions it must answer
- Which other spokes it relates to
- Which service or product page it should lead towards

**Spoke types that commonly work:**
- Cost and pricing
- Comparisons
- Industry or use-case applications
- How-to and implementation
- Problems and mistakes
- Definitions and explainers

---

## Step 6: Plan Internal Linking

Internal links make the cluster a cluster.

**Linking rules:**
- **Pillar → every spoke**, with descriptive anchor text
- **Every spoke → pillar**
- **Spoke → related spokes** where genuinely relevant
- **Pillar and commercial spokes → service or product pages**
- **Existing relevant pages → new cluster pages**

**Anchor text:** descriptive and natural — "WhatsApp chatbot pricing in Pakistan" rather than "click here" or an exact-match keyword repeated everywhere.

Create an internal link matrix in your spreadsheet: rows for source pages, columns for destination pages.

---

## Step 7: Decide Publishing Order

**Recommended sequence:**
1. **Commercial spokes closest to a purchase** — pricing, comparison, service-adjacent pages
2. **Pillar page** — once enough spokes exist to link to
3. **Supporting informational spokes**
4. **Industry and use-case spokes**

Some teams publish the pillar first. Either works, as long as the pillar is updated with links as spokes go live.

**Pace:** consistent publishing over weeks or months, rather than one burst followed by silence.

---

## Step 8: Audit Existing Content

Before creating new pages, check what already exists.

- **Overlapping articles** targeting the same intent — merge them
- **Outdated articles** — update and fold into the cluster
- **Orphan pages** — link them into the cluster
- **Thin pages** — expand, merge or remove

Consolidation often produces faster gains than new content, because existing pages already have history and links.

---

## Step 9: Measure Cluster Performance

Measure the cluster as a unit, not only page by page.

- Total impressions and clicks across cluster pages
- Number of queries the cluster ranks for
- Rankings for the pillar and priority spokes
- Internal link click-through to service pages
- Enquiries and conversions attributed to cluster pages
- Signs of cannibalisation — multiple cluster pages ranking for the same query

Review quarterly: fill gaps, update pages, merge overlaps.

## Common Mistakes

- **Topics too broad to cover properly**
- **Separate pages for keyword variations** with the same intent
- **Pillar pages that duplicate spokes**
- **Weak or missing internal links**
- **No path to commercial pages**
- **Starting several clusters at once**
- **Ignoring existing overlapping content**

## Conclusion

Building a content cluster is a disciplined process: choose a revenue-relevant topic, collect real questions, group them by intent, design a pillar and focused spokes, link them deliberately, publish in a sensible order and measure the cluster as a whole.

Done properly, a cluster builds topical depth that lifts every page within it — and routes readers towards what you sell.

## FAQ

**What is a content cluster in SEO?**
A group of interlinked pages covering a topic in depth, organised around a pillar page and supporting spoke pages.

**How many spoke pages does a cluster need?**
As many as there are distinct search intents worth covering — often between several and a few dozen.

**Should keyword variations get separate pages?**
Only when search results show different intent. Otherwise, combine them.

**Which should be published first, the pillar or spokes?**
Either, provided the pillar is updated with links as spokes are published.

**How long do content clusters take to show results?**
Typically several months, depending on competition and site authority.

**Can existing blog posts become a cluster?**
Yes. Auditing and consolidating existing content is often the fastest starting point.
