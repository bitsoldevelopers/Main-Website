## H1: Lead Generation for Solar Companies in Pakistan

Solar has an unusual cost structure for a consumer purchase: qualifying a lead properly requires a technician to visit a property. That makes the cost of a bad lead far higher than in most industries — it is not a wasted phone call, it is half a day and a vehicle.

Which means the entire lead generation question here is really a filtering question.

## The Channels, Honestly

| Channel | Lead quality | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Meta Ads** | Volume, weak intent | Moderate | Needs heavy qualification |
| **Google Ads** | High intent | Expensive per click | People actively searching to buy |
| **Google Maps / local** | High intent, underused | Effort only | Strong for area-based installers |
| **Organic search** | Best informed | Time | Net metering and cost queries |
| **Referrals** | Highest conversion | Near zero | Systematically neglected |
| **Distributor and dealer networks** | Variable | Commission | Depends on relationship |
| **Society and community groups** | Very high trust | Effort | Underused and genuinely effective |

The last row deserves attention. In Pakistani housing societies, one visible installation generates neighbour enquiries, and community WhatsApp groups spread recommendations faster than advertising reaches them. Installers who deliberately cultivate this outperform those buying leads.

## The Metric That Matters

Not cost per lead. **Site visits per closed sale.**

A company generating cheap leads that require four visits per sale is spending more per customer than one generating expensive leads requiring one and a half. Cost per lead hides this entirely.

**Measure it before changing anything:** count site visits conducted and sales closed over a quarter. That ratio is your actual efficiency, and it is where the improvement lives.

## Filtering Before the Visit

Six things establish whether a lead is real, and all can be asked in chat:

1. **Monthly electricity bill** — the single best proxy for system size and affordability
2. **Property type and ownership** — tenants and apartment residents usually cannot proceed
3. **Roof type and rough area**
4. **Timeframe** — buying now or researching for next summer
5. **Budget awareness** — do they understand the order of magnitude
6. **On-grid, off-grid or hybrid** — different systems, different costs

The bill amount does most of the work. It is how Pakistani customers describe their situation, they answer it readily, and it immediately separates a serious residential prospect from someone with a modest bill expecting a large system.

## What Solar Buyers Are Actually Searching

**Cost queries.** "Solar system price in Pakistan", "10kw solar system cost". Enormous volume, and most installers deflect rather than answer.

**Net metering queries.** Process, timeline, whether it is worth it, DISCO-specific questions. Heavily searched, poorly served.

**Payback and savings queries.** The most searched and the most dangerous to answer carelessly — see below.

**Brand and equipment queries.** Panel and inverter comparisons, warranty questions, genuine versus counterfeit concerns.

**Local installer queries.** "Solar installation in Faisalabad", "solar company near me".

Content addressing the first four brings in informed buyers who arrive already understanding what they are purchasing — which shortens the sales cycle substantially.

## The Claims Problem

Solar marketing in Pakistan has a credibility problem, and it creates both regulatory and reputational risk.

**Do not:**
- Promise a specific payback period. It depends on tariffs that change and consumption that varies.
- Guarantee savings figures.
- Claim net metering approval timelines you do not control.
- Present generic or stock installations as your own work.
- Make equipment claims — efficiency, warranty terms, origin — you cannot substantiate.

**Do:** explain how payback is calculated and what affects it, show your actual installations, be specific about the equipment you supply, and state clearly what depends on the distribution company rather than on you.

Buyers in this market have heard inflated promises before. Precision is a differentiator.

## Nurture, Because the Cycle Is Long

A meaningful share of solar enquiries are people researching for next summer, or waiting for a tariff decision, or saving.

Most installers discard these. That is a mistake — the purchase is high value and the decision is deferred rather than abandoned.

**A workable nurture approach:** periodic, genuinely useful content — net metering process updates, honest cost explanations, completed installation examples in their area — rather than repeated sales messages. Then a check-in when the season turns.

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**Demand spikes with tariff increases and summer outages.** These are partially predictable, and capacity should be planned around them rather than being overwhelmed.

**Exchange rate volatility affects pricing directly.** Panel and inverter costs move with the dollar and import conditions, which is why fixed quotes have short validity and why automated quoting is unsafe.

**Financing and instalment plans** are a major decision factor. Accurate current information about available options materially affects conversion.

**Agricultural and tube well solarisation** is a distinct segment with different sizing, different economics and different buyers. Treat it as a separate campaign, not a variation.

**Trust concerns around equipment quality** are widespread. Transparency about panel and inverter brands, and about what warranties actually cover, converts better than price competition.

**Society-level clustering.** One installation in a housing society generates neighbour interest. Ask for referrals at handover; almost nobody does.

## A Sensible Allocation

For a company currently dependent on paid leads:

1. **Measure site visits per sale.** Establishes the baseline everything is judged against.
2. **Add pre-visit qualification.** Costs nothing, improves every channel immediately.
3. **Claim the Google Business Profile.** High intent, free, underused by installers.
4. **Systematise referrals** — at handover, from every completed installation.
5. **Publish cost and net metering content.** Attracts informed buyers and reduces sales cycle length.
6. **Then judge paid spend** against the improved baseline.

Most companies start and end at step six.

## FAQ

**Which channel produces the best solar leads?**
Google Ads and organic search for intent; referrals and society clustering for conversion. Meta produces volume that requires heavy qualification.

**What should we measure?**
Site visits per closed sale. Cost per lead hides the real cost of unqualified enquiries.

**Should we publish system prices?**
Publish how pricing works and what drives it. Fixed quotes have short validity given exchange rate exposure.

**Can we advertise payback periods?**
No. Payback depends on tariffs and consumption that change. Explain how it is calculated instead.

**How do we qualify before a site visit?**
Bill amount, property type and ownership, roof, timeframe and budget awareness — all askable in chat.

**Are research-stage leads worth keeping?**
Yes. Solar decisions are deferred rather than abandoned, and most competitors discard them entirely.

**What is the most underused channel?**
Referrals from completed installations, and the neighbour interest a visible installation generates in a housing society.
