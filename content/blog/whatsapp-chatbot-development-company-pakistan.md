## H1: WhatsApp Chatbot Development Company in Pakistan: How the Build Actually Works

Most WhatsApp chatbot projects that disappoint were scoped as a chatbot. They should have been scoped as a change to how enquiries are handled — of which the bot is one component, and rarely the hardest one.

This page sets out what a development engagement actually contains, in order, and what to verify at each stage before releasing payment.

---

## Before Anything Is Built

Three things must be settled, and a developer who skips them is planning rework.

**Which number.** A WhatsApp Business API number cannot also be used in the consumer WhatsApp app. If your business has been running on a personal number for years, decide deliberately: migrate it (and lose app access on that number) or start a new one and run a transition period.

**Which access route.** API access is obtained through Meta's Cloud API directly or through a Business Solution Provider. The route affects cost structure, messaging limits and who holds the relationship. Ask which they are using and why.

**What the bot must not do.** Escalation rules written down before the build, not discovered afterwards. Complaints, refunds and anything medical, legal or financial should reach a person quickly.

## Build Phases

### Phase 1 — Conversation design

The actual product. Mapping what customers ask, in what order, and what a good answer looks like. Done properly, this comes from reading your real message history rather than from imagination.

**Verify:** ask to see the conversation map before development starts. If there isn't one, the bot is being improvised.

### Phase 2 — Knowledge and logic

Loading your genuine pricing, services, policies and availability, then defining qualification logic — what the bot needs to establish before a lead is worth a salesperson's time.

**Verify:** check the source material. A bot answering from outdated pricing damages trust faster than no bot at all.

### Phase 3 — Integrations

Where the conversation connects to everything else: CRM, calendar, order system, sheets, payment links, ticketing.

**Verify:** confirm each integration is two-way where it needs to be. A bot that writes to a CRM nobody reads has moved the problem rather than solved it.

### Phase 4 — Testing on real traffic patterns

The phase most often skipped. Test messages in English, Urdu script and Roman Urdu, with local abbreviations, misspellings, voice notes and images — because that is what arrives.

**Verify:** test it yourself, in your customers' language mix, and try to break it. A bot that handles only clean English will fail on most genuine Pakistani enquiries.

### Phase 5 — Template approval and go-live

Message templates for anything sent outside the customer-initiated window require Meta approval. Approvals can be rejected and resubmitted, so this belongs early in the timeline rather than on launch day.

## What to Verify Before Final Payment

- The escalation path works, and a real person receives the handoff
- Conversation history is stored somewhere you can access
- You own the WhatsApp Business Account and the Meta Business Manager asset
- Someone on your team can edit responses without the developer
- Monthly running cost is documented, separate from the build fee
- Fallback behaviour when the bot does not understand is sensible, not a loop

That fourth point decides whether the system stays useful. A bot that requires a developer for every wording change gets abandoned within months.

---

## Realistic Timelines

A focused first build — capture, answer common questions, qualify, hand off — is typically a few weeks, with template approval as the main external dependency. Deep CRM, ERP or payment integrations extend it substantially.

Projects that overrun usually did so for one of two reasons: the business could not supply accurate content about its own services, or the scope grew during the build. Both are avoidable by fixing scope and content before Phase 1.

## Pakistan-Specific Build Considerations

**Bilingual handling is a requirement, not a feature.** Roman Urdu is how a large share of customers write. This must be tested, not assumed.

**Voice notes are common.** Decide early whether the system transcribes them or routes them to a human. Ignoring them means ignoring real enquiries.

**Cash on delivery workflows.** Order confirmation and address verification are high-value automation targets in Pakistani e-commerce and need designing into the flow.

**Business hours and prayer times.** Configure genuine availability, and make the after-hours experience good — that is when the bot earns most of its value.

**Number reputation matters.** Sending bulk template messages to people who did not opt in leads to blocks and quality-rating downgrades that damage delivery for everyone in your account. Any developer proposing bulk unsolicited messaging is putting your number at risk.

## FAQ

**Do I need the WhatsApp Business API for a chatbot?**
Yes. The free Business app supports only basic away messages and quick replies, not genuine automation or integrations.

**Can I keep my existing WhatsApp number?**
You can migrate it, but that number can no longer be used in the consumer app afterwards. Many businesses run a transition period with both.

**How much does a WhatsApp chatbot cost?**
Two figures: the build, and the monthly running cost driven by message volume and platform fees. Insist on both being quoted separately.

**How long does it take to build?**
A focused first build is typically weeks. Template approval and deep integrations are the usual timeline risks.

**Will it handle Urdu and Roman Urdu?**
Modern models handle both reasonably, but quality depends on testing. Test it in your customers' actual language mix before accepting the build.

**What happens when the bot cannot answer?**
It should hand off to a person cleanly and quickly. Verify this works before go-live.

**Can I send marketing messages to my customer list?**
Only to people who opted in, using approved templates. Unsolicited bulk messaging damages your number's quality rating and delivery.
