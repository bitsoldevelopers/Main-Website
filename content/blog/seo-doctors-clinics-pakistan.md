## H1: SEO for Doctors and Clinics in Pakistan

Healthcare search carries a requirement most sectors do not. Google classifies medical content as YMYL — Your Money or Your Life — and applies notably higher standards to who wrote it, what qualifies them, and whether the site is a credible source.

Which is good news for actual doctors. The bar that keeps content farms out is one a real practice can clear simply by being what it already is. Most Pakistani practices fail to rank not because the bar is high, but because they never tell Google or patients who is behind the site.

---

## How Patients Actually Search

Four distinct patterns, needing four different pages:

**Symptom searches** — "pait dard ka ilaj", "chest pain causes". Highest volume, lowest commercial intent, highest responsibility. Requires genuinely careful content.

**Condition and treatment searches** — "hepatitis C treatment in Pakistan", "root canal cost Lahore". Commercial intent, and where cost queries live.

**Doctor searches** — "best dermatologist in Lahore", "child specialist near me", or a doctor's name directly. The highest-intent traffic available to a practice.

**Logistics searches** — timings, fees, panel acceptance, location. Frequently resolved entirely in the local pack.

The mistake most practices make is building only for the fourth, then wondering why nobody arrives.

---

## Doctor Profile Pages Are the Highest-Value Asset

Patients search for named doctors more than practices expect, and a proper profile page satisfies both search and the E-E-A-T requirement simultaneously.

Each doctor needs their own page containing:

- Full name and qualifications, written out
- PMDC registration number
- Specialisation and sub-specialisation
- Years of practice and where trained
- Hospital affiliations
- Conditions and procedures handled
- Clinic timings and fee
- Languages spoken
- Photograph
- A way to book

This is also what establishes authorship credibility for any medical content on the site. Content attributed to a named, registered doctor with a real profile page is treated very differently from unattributed content.

## Condition Content, Done Responsibly

This is where practices can genuinely outrank content farms, and where care is required.

**Do:**
- Attribute every medical article to a named doctor with visible credentials
- Include a reviewed-by line and a date
- Explain the condition in plain language, in the terms patients actually use
- Be explicit about when someone should see a doctor
- Reference established medical sources
- Update periodically and show the date

**Do not:**
- Publish unattributed medical content
- Promise outcomes or cure rates
- Include patient details or images without documented consent
- Make comparative claims about being the best
- Invent statistics — a serious problem in Pakistani health content
- Use `MedicalWebPage` schema on content no clinician reviewed

The honest framing: if a doctor at your practice did not review it, do not publish it as medical content.

## Local SEO Usually Matters More Than Organic

For most Pakistani practices, the local pack drives more appointments than website rankings do. A patient searching "dentist near me" in Johar Town is choosing from three map results.

Priorities:
- Correct primary category — "Dentist", "Dermatologist", "Diagnostic Center", matched to what ranking competitors use
- Accurate pin, verified on a phone, especially for clinics inside plazas or hospitals
- Complete services list
- Accurate timings, including Ramadan special hours
- Genuine review flow with replies
- Individual profiles for practices with multiple doctors at one location, configured carefully so they do not suppress each other

## Reviews in Healthcare: Handle With Care

Reviews matter enormously and carry unique risk.

- **Never respond publicly with clinical detail.** Confirming someone was a patient, or referencing their treatment, breaches confidentiality even when the review is unfair.
- **A safe negative-review reply** acknowledges the concern, apologises for the experience, and moves the conversation offline with a contact point. No specifics.
- **Never incentivise reviews.** Policy violation, and in healthcare an ethical problem.
- **Ask at the right moment** — after successful treatment, not during.

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**Roman Urdu symptom searches are substantial.** Patients search "pait dard", "sar dard", "sugar ka ilaj". Content written only in clinical English misses a large share of real queries. Address the vocabulary patients actually use while remaining medically accurate.

**Panel and insurance queries are high volume.** Which panels you accept is among the most searched practical questions in Pakistani private healthcare, and most practice sites do not answer it.

**Fee transparency.** Consultation fees and procedure costs are searched heavily. Publishing them captures high-intent traffic and reduces reception call volume.

**Female doctor availability** is a genuine and frequent search consideration. State it clearly where applicable.

**Hospital-affiliated doctors** practising at multiple locations need this reflected accurately — patients search by doctor and by hospital.

**Diagnostic labs** have distinct queries around test names, prices, home sampling and report timelines. These are straightforward, high-volume wins that most labs ignore.

## What to Build, in Order

1. **Google Business Profile**, complete and accurate — fastest return
2. **Doctor profile pages**, one per practitioner
3. **Service and procedure pages** with fees where possible
4. **Logistics content** — timings, panels, location, booking
5. **Condition content**, doctor-attributed, starting with what you actually treat most
6. **Review process**, run consistently

Most practices start at step five. Steps one and two produce appointments sooner.

## FAQ

**Can a clinic publish medical content safely?**
Yes, when attributed to a named, qualified clinician who reviewed it, with a visible review date and no outcome promises.

**Is local SEO more important than website SEO for clinics?**
For most practices, yes. The local pack drives appointment volume, particularly for "near me" searches.

**Should we publish consultation fees?**
Generally yes. Patients search cost terms heavily, and transparency captures intent while reducing reception calls.

**How do we respond to a negative review?**
Acknowledge, apologise for the experience, and move offline. Never confirm treatment details or that the person was a patient.

**Do doctors need individual pages?**
Yes. Patients search doctors by name, and profiles establish the credibility that medical content requires.

**Can we write in Roman Urdu?**
Address the vocabulary patients use while keeping content medically accurate. Symptom searches in Roman Urdu are substantial.

**What about patient testimonials?**
Only with documented written consent, and never with clinical detail the patient did not agree to publish.
