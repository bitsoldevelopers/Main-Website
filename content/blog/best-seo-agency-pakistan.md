## H1: Best SEO Agency in Pakistan: How to Evaluate One Before You Pay

SEO is the easiest marketing service to sell badly. The work is invisible to the client, results take months, and by the time it is obvious that nothing is happening, six months of retainer is gone.

That asymmetry is why so many Pakistani businesses have been burned by SEO — and why the useful question is not "who is the best SEO agency in Pakistan" but "how do I tell, in one meeting, whether this agency does real work."

**Short answer:** ask to see the deliverables, not the results. Real SEO produces artefacts — technical audits with prioritised fixes, keyword-to-page mapping, content briefs, internal link plans, before-and-after crawl data. An agency that cannot show you these, anonymised, from current clients is selling reports rather than rankings.

## What SEO Work Actually Consists Of

If you cannot recognise the work, you cannot judge the worker. Legitimate SEO has four workstreams, and a serious proposal will name all four.

### Technical SEO

Making the site crawlable, indexable and fast. Fixing broken canonicals, redirect chains, duplicate URLs, orphaned pages, blocked resources, structured data errors, and Core Web Vitals.

**What the deliverable looks like:** a crawl report with issues ranked by impact, each with a specific fix and an owner.

### On-page and content

Mapping keywords to pages so each page owns one intent, writing or rewriting content to satisfy that intent, and structuring headings, titles and internal links accordingly.

**What the deliverable looks like:** a keyword-to-URL map and content briefs with target queries, word counts and section outlines.

### Off-page and authority

Earning links and mentions from real sites. In Pakistan this often means industry directories, chambers, trade associations, local press, partnerships and genuine PR.

**What the deliverable looks like:** a list of acquired links with the referring domain, the page, and how it was earned. Not a spreadsheet of 500 forum profiles.

### Local and entity signals

For any business with a location or service area: Google Business Profile, NAP consistency, citations, review flow, and `LocalBusiness` schema.

**What the deliverable looks like:** profile audit, corrected listings, and a map-based visibility baseline.

## The Fastest Way to Audit a Proposal

Read the proposal against this table. Most weak proposals fail three or more rows immediately.

| Proposal contains | Good sign | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Keyword list | Mapped to specific URLs and intents | A flat list of 50 keywords with no page assignment |
| Timeline | Phased, with month-one technical work | "Results in 30 days" |
| Reporting | Named metrics, named tool, named cadence | "Monthly report" with no specifics |
| Link building | Method described, quality criteria stated | A monthly link quota with no sourcing detail |
| Content | Briefs, topics, and who writes them | "Blog posts: 4/month" and nothing else |
| Access | Requests GSC, GA4 and site access | Does not ask for Search Console |

That last row is the quiet giveaway. An agency that does not ask for Google Search Console access in week one is not planning to look at your actual search data.

## Guarantees, and Why They Signal Trouble

No one controls Google's ranking systems. An agency guaranteeing a position is doing one of three things:

- **Targeting keywords nobody searches.** Ranking first for "affordable AI marketing agency in Model Town Lahore" is trivial and worthless.
- **Guaranteeing something unmeasurable.** "Improved visibility" cannot be failed.
- **Planning to blame you.** When the guarantee fails, the reason becomes your website, your content approvals, or your competitors.

What an agency *can* legitimately commit to: a defined volume of work, a technical health threshold, publishing cadence, response times, and reporting standards. Commit to inputs and process, measure outcomes honestly.

---

## Reporting Standards Worth Insisting On

A monthly report should let you answer three questions without calling anyone.

1. **Is qualified organic traffic growing?** Sessions from search to pages that matter — not total sessions inflated by brand searches or bot traffic.
2. **Are we winning the queries we chose?** Position and impression trends for the mapped keyword set, from Search Console, not a third-party rank tracker checking from one location.
3. **What was actually done, and what is next?** A list of shipped work, not a list of activities.

For local businesses, add map visibility measured across a grid of points rather than a single check — Google Maps results change with the searcher's location, so one rank check tells you almost nothing.

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**Verify before you trust the case study.** Ask for the live URL of a client site and check it yourself. Search a few of their claimed keywords. Anonymised is fine; unverifiable is not.

**Content in the right register.** A lot of SEO content produced for Pakistani sites reads as though it were written for a US audience, or as though translated. It underperforms because it does not match how local buyers phrase things — including Roman Urdu, neighbourhood names, and landmark-based location language.

**Link building shortcuts are widespread.** Cheap link packages built on PBNs, comment spam and paid directory dumps are common in the market and carry real risk. Ask directly how links are acquired, and be suspicious of volume promises.

**Maps often matters more than the website.** For clinics, salons, restaurants, academies, dealers and service businesses, local pack visibility can drive more revenue than organic rankings. Confirm it is in scope.

**Check the existing site first.** Many Pakistani SME sites have fundamental problems — no indexable content, JavaScript-only rendering, missing sitemaps, duplicate city pages. SEO on a broken site is money spent on scaffolding.

## What to Expect Month by Month

A realistic engagement, assuming a site in average condition:

1. **Month 1** — Technical audit, Search Console and analytics setup, keyword-to-page mapping, priority fixes begin. Little visible ranking movement.
2. **Month 2–3** — Technical fixes shipped, first content published or rewritten, local profile corrected. Impressions typically move before positions do.
3. **Month 3–4** — Early ranking movement on lower-competition and long-tail queries. Traffic curve begins to bend.
4. **Month 5–6** — Competitive terms start moving. This is the first honest point at which to judge the engagement.
5. **Beyond** — Compounding. Content already published continues to accumulate authority.

If someone shows you a curve that goes vertical in week three, they are showing you paid traffic, brand search, or someone else's account.

## FAQ

**How much does SEO cost in Pakistan?**
It varies with site condition, competition and scope. Insist on a fee itemised by deliverable so you can compare proposals meaningfully rather than by headline price.

**How long does SEO take to work in Pakistan?**
Typically three to six months for meaningful movement on competitive terms, sometimes faster for long-tail and local queries. Technical fixes can show results sooner.

**Can any agency guarantee first-page rankings?**
No. Nobody controls Google's ranking systems. Treat guarantees as a signal to look elsewhere.

**Should I hire an SEO agency or an in-house specialist?**
In-house suits businesses with continuous content needs and a large site. An agency suits businesses needing a broader skill mix — technical, content, local and links — without hiring four people.

**What if my website is old and badly built?**
Fix the foundation first. SEO on a site that cannot be crawled or loaded properly is spending on work that cannot compound.

**Do I need SEO if I already run Google Ads?**
They serve different functions. Ads stop the moment budget stops; organic visibility persists. Most businesses run both, with ads funding pipeline while organic is built.

**How do I know the links they built are safe?**
Ask for the full list with referring domains, then look at the sites yourself. If you would not want your business associated with a page, Google likely agrees.
