## H1: SEO for Pakistani E-commerce Websites

Most e-commerce SEO effort in Pakistan goes into product pages. Most e-commerce organic traffic arrives on category pages.

That mismatch explains a lot of disappointing results. Someone searching "kurta for men" or "office chair price in pakistan" is not looking for one product — they want a selection. The page that satisfies that intent is a well-built category page, and it is the page most Pakistani stores neglect entirely.

---

## Category Pages Are the Asset

A category page competing for a commercial term needs more than a grid of products.

**What it must have:**
- A descriptive H1 matching the search term
- Genuine introductory content — what the range covers, how to choose, price bands, materials or specifications
- Sensible filtering that does not fragment the page into thousands of crawlable variants
- Internal links to related and sub-categories
- Content that answers the questions a buyer has before choosing

**What most Pakistani store category pages have:** a title, a product grid, and nothing else. Which is why marketplaces outrank them for almost every commercial term.

The practical exercise: search your top five commercial keywords, look at what ranks, and note that the winning pages almost always contain buying guidance, not only inventory.

---

## Product Pages: What Actually Matters

- **Unique descriptions.** Manufacturer copy duplicated across every store competing on the same product is the single most common product-page problem.
- **Complete specifications** — dimensions, material, fabric, warranty, compatibility. Pakistani buyers ask these on WhatsApp precisely because pages omit them.
- **Product schema** with price, availability and currency in PKR.
- **Real images**, optimised. Multiple angles, correct dimensions, lazy loaded below the fold.
- **Reviews**, where genuine. Never fabricate them and never mark up review schema you do not have.
- **Clear delivery and return information** on the page itself, because it is a purchase blocker in this market.

## Faceted Navigation: The Silent Index Killer

Filters — colour, size, price, brand — generate URL combinations. Left uncontrolled, a store with a few hundred products generates tens of thousands of crawlable URLs, most of them near-duplicates.

The consequences are real: crawl budget wasted on filter permutations, duplicate content dilution, and important pages crawled infrequently.

**Control it:**
- Decide which facets deserve indexable pages — usually a small number with genuine search demand, such as a brand or a well-searched colour
- `noindex` the rest, or block the parameter patterns
- Canonical filtered variants to the parent category where appropriate
- Keep filter URLs consistent in parameter order

This is unglamorous and it is frequently the highest-impact technical work on a Pakistani e-commerce site.

---

## Out-of-Stock and Discontinued Products

A recurring problem, and mishandling it destroys accumulated ranking.

- **Temporarily out of stock:** keep the page live, mark availability correctly in schema, offer notification or alternatives. Do not delete it.
- **Permanently discontinued:** redirect to the closest equivalent product or the parent category. Do not leave it 404ing if it had traffic or links.
- **Seasonal items:** keep the page and update it, rather than removing and recreating each year.

Stores that delete out-of-stock products rebuild their rankings from zero every season.

## Competing Against Marketplaces

Pakistani e-commerce search results are dominated by large marketplaces with authority you will not match directly. Competing head-on for broad terms is usually a losing allocation.

**Where an independent store can win:**

| Approach | Why it works |
|---|---|
| **Long-tail and specific terms** | Marketplaces optimise for broad; specific product and use-case queries are winnable |
| **Depth of information** | Marketplace listings are thin. Genuine buying guides, sizing help and comparisons outrank them on informational-commercial queries |
| **Brand search** | Build a brand people search by name; that traffic converts best and cannot be taken |
| **Local intent** | If you have a physical presence, Maps and local search are channels marketplaces cannot occupy |
| **Category authority** | Being the obvious specialist in one narrow category beats being a worse version of a general marketplace |

## Pakistan-Specific Technical Realities

**Mobile performance is a revenue factor.** Most traffic arrives on mid-range Android over variable connections. Heavy product galleries and large JavaScript bundles lose buyers before the page renders. Test on a real device on mobile data, not office broadband.

**Cash on delivery affects the content, not just operations.** Buyers want to know COD availability, delivery timeframe to their city, and return process before purchasing. Putting this on the page reduces both abandonment and WhatsApp enquiry load.

**Price in PKR, correctly marked up.** Schema with the wrong currency produces wrong rich results.

**Bilingual product names.** Many products are searched by both English and Urdu or transliterated names — the same garment or spice may have several common spellings. Reflect real search language in titles and descriptions.

**City-level delivery information** is searched. "Delivery to Multan", "COD available in Peshawar" are genuine queries for stores shipping nationally.

**WhatsApp is the pre-purchase channel.** Expect and plan for buyers asking questions before ordering. Good page content reduces the volume; automation handles the rest.

## A Practical Priority Order

1. **Fix crawl and index control** — faceted navigation, duplicates, sitemap accuracy
2. **Build out category pages** for your highest-value commercial terms
3. **Fix product schema** — price, availability, currency
4. **Improve mobile performance**, measured on a real device
5. **Rewrite duplicated product descriptions**, highest-traffic first
6. **Add buying-guide content** targeting informational-commercial queries
7. **Build brand and category authority** through content and genuine mentions

Most stores start at step six. Steps one to three usually produce faster returns.

## FAQ

**Should I focus on product or category pages?**
Category pages for commercial traffic; product pages for specific-item and long-tail searches. Most stores underinvest in categories.

**How do I stop filters creating thousands of URLs?**
Decide which facets deserve indexing, `noindex` or block the rest, and canonical filtered variants to the parent category.

**What should I do with out-of-stock products?**
Keep the page live with correct availability schema if it is returning. Redirect to the nearest equivalent if discontinued. Do not delete.

**Can an independent store outrank a marketplace?**
On broad terms, rarely. On specific products, buying guides, brand terms and local intent, yes.

**Does Shopify or WooCommerce rank better?**
Neither inherently. Configuration, performance and content determine outcomes.

**How important is page speed for e-commerce here?**
Very. Most Pakistani traffic is mobile on variable connections, and slow product pages lose buyers before rendering.

**Should product descriptions be in English or Urdu?**
Match how your buyers search. Many products are searched under both, and transliterated spellings vary — reflect real usage.
