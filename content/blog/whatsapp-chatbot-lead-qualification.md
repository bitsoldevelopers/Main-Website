## H1: WhatsApp Chatbot for Lead Qualification

Qualification is the highest-value thing automation does in a Pakistani sales process, and the easiest to overdo.

Ask too little and salespeople spend their week discovering that half their conversations were never going to buy. Ask too much and genuine buyers abandon a conversation that started to feel like a form.

The design problem is finding the smallest set of questions that reliably separates the two.

---

## Start From Your Actual Losses

Do not design qualification from a template. Look at your last thirty enquiries that consumed sales time and did not close, and work out what they had in common.

Typically it is one of a small number of things:
- Budget was never in range
- Wrong city or outside your service area
- Not the decision maker
- Timeframe was months away
- Wanted something you do not offer
- Not a real enquiry at all

Whichever two or three of these account for most of your wasted time — those are your qualification questions. Everything else is optional.

---

## The Four That Usually Suffice

**What do you need, specifically?** Not the category — the actual thing. This alone eliminates people wanting something adjacent that you do not sell.

**Where are you?** City or area. Cheap to ask, and a hard disqualifier for anyone outside your service range.

**What budget range are you working with?** The question sellers avoid and buyers answer readily. Offering ranges rather than asking for a number makes it comfortable.

**When are you looking to proceed?** Separates this month from next year — not a disqualifier, but it determines routing.

A fifth is sometimes warranted: **who else is involved in the decision?** Useful in B2B and for family purchases, which in Pakistan covers property, vehicles, education and weddings.

---

## Order Matters More Than People Expect

**Easy before hard.** Start with what they want, which they are eager to answer. Budget comes third or fourth, once they have invested in the conversation.

**Give before you take.** Answer something useful first — a price range, a process explanation. A conversation that only extracts feels like an interrogation.

**Explain why you are asking.** "So I can send you the right options" makes budget questions land as helpfulness rather than gatekeeping.

**Never open with budget.** It reads as filtering, and it is the fastest way to lose a serious buyer who has not yet decided you are worth talking to.

---

## Offer Ranges, Not Open Questions

Two design details that materially affect completion:

**Ranges over numbers.** "Under 5 lakh / 5–10 lakh / above 10 lakh" gets answered far more often than "what is your budget?" People are comfortable placing themselves in a band and uncomfortable stating a figure.

**Buttons over free text** where the answer set is known. Faster for the customer, cleaner for you, and it removes parsing ambiguity.

Free text belongs where the answer genuinely varies — describing a requirement, a problem, a property.

---

## What to Do With Leads That Fail

This is where most implementations waste money.

**Out of area:** offer an alternative if you have one — a partner, a different service. Do not simply end the conversation.

**Below budget:** offer your entry option if one exists. A meaningful share of "budget too low" enquiries convert to a smaller purchase.

**Too early:** nurture sequence. Someone researching for next year is not a bad lead, just an early one — and most Pakistani businesses discard them entirely.

**Not the decision maker:** ask to be connected, politely. In family purchases the person messaging is frequently gathering information for someone else.

**Genuinely wrong fit:** decline warmly and briefly. It costs nothing and people remember it.

Discarding every unqualified lead throws away a real percentage of eventual revenue.

---

## How Many Questions Is Too Many

Abandonment rises sharply with length. Practical guidance:

- **Four questions** is the comfortable ceiling for most consumer enquiries
- **Five or six** is tolerable for high-value considered purchases — property, vehicles, education
- **Beyond six**, expect meaningful drop-off unless the customer is highly motivated

If you believe you need eight questions, you are probably trying to complete the sale in qualification. Establish enough to route correctly and let the salesperson do the rest.

---

## Pakistan-Specific Design Notes

**Roman Urdu answers.** Budget arrives as "5 lakh tak", area as "Johar Town", timeframe as "abhi chahiye". The system must parse these, not just clean English.

**Family decision-making.** For property, vehicles, education and weddings the person messaging is often not the decision maker. Qualification should surface this without being intrusive.

**Budget sensitivity is high but answerable.** Pakistani buyers state budget more readily than sellers expect, particularly when given ranges.

**Evening enquiries need the same qualification.** The value is that a lead arriving at 11pm is qualified and routed by morning rather than starting from zero.

**Voice notes.** Many customers answer by voice. Decide whether to transcribe or route.

**Trust matters at the start.** Disclose that this is an automated assistant and a person is available. Concealment discovered mid-qualification ends the conversation.

---

## Measuring Whether It Works

- **Completion rate** — what share who start qualification finish it. Falling completion means too many questions.
- **Qualified leads as a share of total enquiries**
- **Conversion rate from qualified lead** — the number that proves your criteria are right
- **Salesperson time per closed deal**
- **Recovered leads** from nurture sequences

If conversion from qualified leads has not improved, your criteria are letting the wrong people through. If completion has dropped, you are asking too much.

## Conclusion

Good qualification asks the fewest questions that reliably separate buyers from browsers, in an order that feels helpful rather than obstructive.

Derive the questions from your own wasted time, offer ranges, keep it to four where possible, and do something useful with the leads that fail. That last part is where most of the recoverable revenue sits.

## FAQ

**How many qualification questions should I ask?**
Four for most consumer enquiries, five or six for high-value considered purchases. Beyond that, completion drops sharply.

**Will asking about budget scare people off?**
Not if it comes third or fourth and offers ranges rather than requesting a figure. Pakistani buyers answer it more readily than sellers expect.

**What should happen to unqualified leads?**
Offer alternatives, entry options, or a nurture sequence. Discarding them throws away real eventual revenue.

**Can it handle Roman Urdu answers?**
It must — budget and area arrive that way routinely. Test on real messages before launch.

**Should qualification happen before or after answering their questions?**
Answer something useful first. A conversation that only extracts information feels like an interrogation.

**How do I know my criteria are right?**
Watch conversion from qualified leads. If it has not improved, the criteria are letting the wrong people through.

**Buttons or free text?**
Buttons where the answer set is known, free text where requirements genuinely vary.
