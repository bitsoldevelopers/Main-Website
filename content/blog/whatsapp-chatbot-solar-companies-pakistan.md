## H1: WhatsApp Chatbot for Solar Companies in Pakistan

Solar in Pakistan has a specific and expensive problem: the site visit. An engineer spends half a day travelling to a property, surveying a roof, and discovering the customer's budget was a third of what the system costs.

Every one of those visits is a real cost — fuel, a technician's day, and a slot that a serious buyer could have had. And almost all of them were avoidable, because the disqualifying information was available in the first conversation if anyone had asked.

## What Determines Whether a Solar Lead Is Real

Six things, all askable in chat, none requiring a visit:

| Question | Why it disqualifies or qualifies |
|---|---|
| **Monthly electricity bill** | The single best proxy for system size and affordability |
| **Property type and ownership** | Owned house, rented, apartment, commercial, agricultural — tenants and apartment residents usually cannot proceed |
| **Roof type and rough area** | Determines feasibility and mounting approach |
| **Timeframe** | Buying this month versus researching for next year |
| **Budget awareness** | Whether they understand the order of magnitude involved |
| **On-grid, off-grid or hybrid interest** | Completely different systems and costs |

The bill amount does most of the work. A customer with a modest bill asking about a large system, or a large bill with a very small budget, is identifiable in one exchange.

## What the Bot Should Do

**Ask for the bill amount first.** It is the fastest qualifier and customers answer it readily.

**Give indicative system sizing.** A rough kW range appropriate to that consumption, clearly labelled as indicative and subject to survey. This genuinely helps the customer and demonstrates competence.

**Explain net metering** — what it is, roughly how the process works, and that timelines depend on the distribution company. This is among the most asked questions in Pakistani solar and is almost always answered manually.

**Set expectations on cost structure** — what drives price, what is included, what is not. Not a firm quote.

**Explain the process** — survey, design, approval, installation, commissioning.

**Capture location** — city and area, since coverage and travel cost vary.

**Book the survey** only once the lead qualifies.

## What Must Reach a Person

- **Final pricing.** Panel and inverter prices move with exchange rates and import conditions. Automated quoting in this market creates disputes.
- **Payback period claims.** Never let automation promise a return timeline — it depends on tariff changes nobody controls.
- **Technical feasibility judgments** on a specific roof.
- **Warranty and after-sales disputes.**
- **Net metering approval promises.** The process depends on the distribution company; do not let a bot commit to it.

That second point matters. Payback promises are the most common overclaim in Pakistani solar marketing, and automating them scales a liability.

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**Load-shedding and tariff anxiety drives demand.** Enquiry volume spikes with electricity price increases and summer outages. These surges are predictable and overwhelming.

**Bill amounts are the shared vocabulary.** Customers do not think in kilowatts; they think in rupees per month. Design the conversation around how they actually describe their situation.

**Net metering is the dominant question.** Process, timeline, and whether it is worth it. DISCO-specific variation means information must be editable by your team as rules change.

**Exchange rate volatility affects pricing directly.** Panel and inverter costs move with the dollar and import conditions. This is precisely why automated quoting is unsafe here.

**Instalment and financing questions are frequent.** Bank financing and instalment plans are a major decision factor. Have accurate current information and route detailed questions to a person.

**Rural and agricultural demand** — tube well solarisation is a distinct segment with different sizing and different economics. Build it as a separate branch rather than forcing it into a residential flow.

**Trust is a live issue.** The sector has had quality problems with panels and installations. Automated responses should be transparent and never overstate certainty.

## A Realistic Flow

1. Enquiry arrives — from a Meta ad, Google search, or Google Business Profile
2. Greeting: automated assistant, human available
3. **Bill amount asked early**
4. Property type and ownership
5. Location and roof description
6. Indicative system size range, clearly caveated
7. Net metering and process explanation
8. Timeframe and budget awareness
9. Qualified leads → survey booked, engineer briefed with full summary
10. Unqualified or researching leads → nurture sequence rather than discard

Step ten matters. A customer researching for next summer is not a bad lead, just an early one. Most solar companies discard them entirely.

## FAQ

**Can it quote a system price?**
It can give indicative sizing and cost drivers. Final pricing should come from your team, because panel and inverter costs move with exchange rates.

**How does it size a system?**
From the monthly bill amount, which is how Pakistani customers describe their consumption. Sizing is indicative and confirmed by survey.

**Can it explain net metering?**
Yes, at a process level — but never promise approval or timelines, which depend on the distribution company.

**Should it discuss payback period?**
No. Payback depends on tariffs that change. Automated payback promises are a liability.

**Will it reduce wasted site visits?**
That is the primary purpose. Track site visits per closed sale before and after.

**What about tube well and agricultural enquiries?**
Build them as a separate branch — sizing and economics differ substantially from residential.

**What should never be automated?**
Final pricing, payback claims, roof feasibility judgments, and warranty disputes.
