## H1: SEO for Solar Companies in Pakistan

Solar in Pakistan has moved from a niche purchase to a mainstream household and business decision. Rising electricity tariffs pushed homeowners, factories, schools and shops to look seriously at rooftop systems — and almost all of them start by searching.

That search behaviour has created a crowded market. Many installers compete for the same customers with near-identical Facebook posts and price lists. The companies that rank in search, and look credible when a buyer checks them, get the calls the others never see.

## How Solar Buyers Search

Solar is a considered purchase with a long research phase. Searches follow a recognisable sequence.

**Cost and payback queries.** "5kW solar system price in Pakistan", "how many years to recover solar cost", "solar vs WAPDA bill". Buyers want numbers before anything else.

**System-size queries.** 3kW, 5kW, 10kW, 15kW, 25kW — often combined with what the system can run: ACs, motors, a whole house.

**Technology queries.** On-grid vs hybrid vs off-grid, inverter brands, panel types, batteries, net metering.

**Process and policy queries.** How net metering works, which documents are required, how long approval takes with the distribution company.

**Local provider queries.** "Solar installation company in Lahore", "solar installer near me", often from Google Maps.

**Commercial and industrial queries.** Factory, school, hospital, cold storage and agricultural tube-well systems — lower volume but far higher value.

Most solar company websites contain a home page, a gallery and a contact form. They target none of these queries directly.

## The Page Architecture That Works

### City and service-area pages

One page per city you genuinely install in — Lahore, Karachi, Islamabad, Rawalpindi, Faisalabad, Multan, Gujranwala, Sialkot, Peshawar. Each should include real installations in that city, local distribution company details for net metering, response times, and service coverage.

Thin pages that swap the city name and nothing else do not rank for long and do not convince buyers.

### System-size pages

A page for each common system size: what it runs, typical daily generation in Pakistani conditions, space required, on-grid versus hybrid options, and an indicative price range with a date stamp.

### Technology comparison pages

On-grid vs hybrid vs off-grid. Lithium vs tubular batteries. Inverter types. Honest trade-offs, not brand promotion.

### Net metering guide

A clear, current explanation of the process, documents, timeline and what the installer handles. This is one of the most searched and least well-answered topics in the sector.

### Commercial and industrial pages

Separate pages for factories, schools, hospitals, agriculture and offices. The buyer, the decision process and the financials differ from residential.

### Project and case study pages

Real installations with photographs, system size, location, equipment used, and — with permission — before-and-after electricity bills.

## Pricing Content: Publish It Carefully

Solar prices in Pakistan move with exchange rates, import costs and panel supply. That tempts companies to publish nothing, which sends buyers to competitors who do.

**A better approach:**
- Publish indicative ranges, clearly dated
- Explain what changes the price — panel brand, inverter type, batteries, structure, cabling, installation complexity
- Update the page when the market moves, and show the update date
- Offer a site survey for an exact quote

Dated, explained ranges build trust. Stale prices from a year ago destroy it.

## Google Maps Is Half the Battle

A large share of solar enquiries come through local search and Google Maps.

- Correct primary category, with secondary categories for relevant services
- An accurate address — showrooms and offices, not a residential address listed as a business
- Genuine photographs of installations, not manufacturer stock images
- Regular review requests after installation and after the first bill cycle, when customers see savings
- Responses to every review, including the difficult ones
- Service areas that match where you actually install

Fake reviews are a known problem in this sector, and buyers are increasingly sceptical of profiles with dozens of identical five-star reviews posted in a week. ## Trust Signals Solar Buyers Check

A solar system is a significant investment with a long warranty period. Buyers want to know you will still exist when something goes wrong.

- **Company registration and physical office address**
- **Named engineers and their qualifications**
- **Equipment warranties** — which are the manufacturer's, and which are yours
- **After-sales service terms** — response times, maintenance, monitoring
- **Authorised dealer status** with brands, where genuine
- **Real installation photographs** with locations
- **Clear explanation of what happens if net metering approval is delayed**

Vague warranty language is the single biggest trust killer in solar marketing.

## FAQ

**Should solar companies publish prices online?**
Yes, as dated indicative ranges with an explanation of what changes the price. It builds trust and qualifies enquiries.

**Do city pages help solar companies rank?**
Yes, when each contains genuine local projects and details. Pages that only swap the city name tend not to hold rankings.

**How important is Google Maps for solar installers?**
Very. Many residential enquiries come from local search, and reviews heavily influence which installer buyers call.

**What content do solar buyers search for most?**
System prices, payback periods, system sizes, on-grid versus hybrid comparisons and net metering guidance.

**Should residential and commercial solar have separate pages?**
Yes. The buyers, decision process and financial case are different.

**How long does solar SEO take?**
Maps improvements can show within weeks. Competitive organic rankings usually take several months.
