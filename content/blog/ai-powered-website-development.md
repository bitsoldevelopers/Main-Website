## H1: AI-Powered Website Development

"AI-powered website" is used for two entirely different things: a site *built* with AI assistance, and a site *containing* AI features. They have almost nothing in common, and conflating them is how businesses end up paying a premium for something they did not want.

Worth separating before you commission anything.

---

## Meaning One: Built With AI Assistance

Developers using AI tools to write code faster. This is now normal practice rather than a feature, and it should not carry a premium.

**What it legitimately changes:** speed of building standard components, boilerplate, and first drafts of content.

**What it does not change:** whether the site is architected sensibly, whether content is indexable, whether it performs on a mid-range phone, or whether anyone can maintain it.

**Where it goes wrong:** AI-generated code ships fast and accumulates decisions nobody understands. Six months later a bug appears and neither the developer nor anyone else can explain why the code does what it does.

**What to ask:** who reviewed this, and can another developer maintain it? Documentation matters more when generation is cheap, not less.

---

## Meaning Two: AI Features On the Site

Actual functionality for visitors. This is where the genuine value sits, and it is worth being specific about which features earn their cost.

### On-site assistant

A chat interface answering questions from your content. Useful for large catalogues, complex service ranges and documentation-heavy sites.

**The caveat for Pakistan:** most visitors would rather message you on WhatsApp than use a website widget. For many Pakistani businesses a well-placed click-to-chat link outperforms an on-site assistant at a fraction of the cost.

### Search that understands intent

For sites with substantial content or large catalogues, search that handles natural phrasing rather than exact keywords. Genuinely useful for e-commerce with hundreds of products; unnecessary for a fifteen-page site.

### Personalisation

Showing different content based on behaviour or source. Real value at scale, and largely wasted below meaningful traffic volume — you need enough visitors for the segments to be worth building.

### Content assistance in the CMS

Draft generation and suggestions for whoever writes your content. Practical and low-risk, provided a person edits before publishing.

### Product recommendations

For e-commerce with sufficient catalogue and transaction history. Below a certain scale, simple rule-based recommendations perform comparably at far lower cost.

## Where AI-Generated Sites Quietly Fail

These are the recurring problems, and they are not obvious at handover:

**Client-side rendering.** Many AI-assisted builds default to rendering content in the browser. The site looks perfect and ships an almost-empty HTML document to crawlers. **Check by viewing the page source and searching for text you can see on screen.**

**Generic content.** AI-generated copy that describes any business in your category rather than yours. It reads competently and converts poorly, because it says nothing specific.

**Accessibility gaps.** Generated markup frequently misses semantic structure, alt text, focus states and keyboard navigation.

**Bloat.** Components generated rather than designed tend to include more than they need. This shows up as poor mobile performance.

**Undocumented decisions.** The most consequential one. Code that works but that nobody can explain becomes unmaintainable at exactly the moment you need to change it.

## What Actually Matters More Than AI

Uncomfortable but true: the things determining whether a website works for a Pakistani business are the same as they were before.

- Content visible in the HTML source
- Fast on a mid-range Android over mobile data
- Clear about what you sell and what it costs
- Contact and WhatsApp reachable in one tap
- Crawlable name, address and phone
- Someone able to update it without a developer
- Redirects mapped if it replaces an existing site

A site with an AI assistant and none of these underperforms a plain site with all of them.

---

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**WhatsApp beats on-site chat.** This is the single most important point for Pakistani sites. Visitors are more comfortable in WhatsApp, the conversation continues after they leave, and you keep the contact. An on-site assistant that does not hand off to WhatsApp is losing the thread.

**Running costs in USD.** On-site AI features carry per-query costs that scale with traffic. Model them before committing, particularly if traffic is growing.

**Bilingual handling.** If visitors ask in Roman Urdu, an assistant trained only on formal English content will fail. Test it on real phrasing.

**Data and device constraints.** AI features add weight. On mid-range phones over mobile data, an assistant widget that delays interactivity costs more than it returns.

**Personalisation needs volume.** Most Pakistani SME sites do not have the traffic for personalisation to pay back. Be honest about whether you do.

## What to Ask a Developer Proposing This

1. Which of the two meanings are you selling me?
2. How will pages be rendered, and will content appear in the HTML source?
3. What is the monthly running cost of any AI features at my traffic?
4. Does the on-site assistant hand off to WhatsApp?
5. Who wrote the content, and who edited it?
6. Can another developer maintain this? Show me the documentation.
7. What did you test on a mid-range Android over mobile data?

Question three catches most of the surprises, and question two catches most of the damage.

## Conclusion

AI in website development is two separate things. Being built with AI assistance is now ordinary and should not cost extra. AI features on the site are worth paying for when your catalogue or content is large enough to need them.

## FAQ

**Is an AI-built website cheaper?**
It can be faster to produce, but the cost of the build is not where most website value or risk sits. Architecture, content and maintainability matter more.

**Are AI-generated websites bad for SEO?**
Not inherently. They fail when content is rendered client-side or when the copy is generic. Both are checkable before you pay.

**Should my site have an AI chatbot?**
In Pakistan, a prominent WhatsApp link usually outperforms an on-site widget. Consider an assistant if you have a large catalogue or complex documentation.

**What does an on-site AI assistant cost to run?**
Per-query charges scaling with traffic, in USD. Get this quoted separately from the build.

**Can AI write my website content?**
It can draft it. Someone who knows the business must edit it, or it will describe your category rather than your company.

**How do I check for client-side rendering?**
View the page source and search for a sentence you can see on screen. If it is absent, content is rendered in the browser.

**Does personalisation work for small sites?**
Rarely. It needs enough traffic for segments to be meaningful. Below that, it is cost without return.
