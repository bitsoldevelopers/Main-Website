## H1: AI Strategy Consulting in Pakistan

Most AI strategy engagements are a sales process wearing a consulting hat. A discovery workshop, a maturity framework, a slide deck, and a recommendation to buy the implementation services of whoever produced it.

Genuine advisory looks different, and the difference is testable before you sign: it produces decisions you could act on with a different supplier, and it is willing to conclude that you should not build anything yet.

---

## What a Real Engagement Produces

**A prioritised list of processes, with numbers.** Not "opportunities for AI" — specific processes, what each currently costs in hours or lost revenue, and what could realistically change. If the deliverable contains no numbers from your business, it was written before anyone looked at your business.

**A build-buy-configure recommendation per item.** Most problems are solved by configuring existing tools. A consultant who recommends custom development for everything is describing their own service catalogue.

**A sequence.** What to do first, what depends on what, and what should wait. Sequencing is most of the value, because doing the right things in the wrong order is how AI budgets get spent without producing anything.

**Honest constraints.** Data you do not have, processes not documented well enough to automate, payment or infrastructure limitations, and capability gaps in your team.

**Cost projections including running costs.** Build and run, separately, modelled at your realistic volume, in the currency you will actually be billed.

**A measurement plan.** How you will know whether any of it worked.

---

## The Tests

**Ask what they would recommend if you had no budget for implementation.** A genuine advisor still has useful answers — process fixes, tool configuration, things your team can do. A sales process in disguise struggles.

**Ask what you should not automate.** Anyone who cannot name several things has not deployed enough systems to have learned.

**Ask whether the deliverable is usable by another supplier.** It should be. Strategy that only makes sense if the strategist implements it is not strategy.

**Ask about failures.** A consultant who has never had a deployment go badly has either not deployed much or is not being candid.

## Scope That Fits a Pakistani Mid-Market Business

International AI strategy engagements are frequently priced and scoped for organisations with data teams, documented processes and mature systems. Most Pakistani businesses have none of these, and a framework built for that context produces recommendations nobody can act on.

**Realistic scope here usually means:**

- Two to four weeks, not six months
- Focused on three to five processes, not an enterprise-wide transformation map
- Assuming spreadsheets and WhatsApp as the current systems, because they usually are
- Accounting for USD-denominated tooling against PKR revenue
- Producing something a team of five can execute

An engagement that concludes you need a data warehouse before you can do anything has correctly identified a constraint and produced an unusable recommendation.

---

## What Usually Comes Back

Across most Pakistani mid-market businesses, the same short list recurs:

1. **Enquiry response and capture** — usually the largest quantifiable loss, and the cheapest to fix
2. **Lead qualification and routing**
3. **Follow-up sequences** — the step nobody does
4. **Document data extraction** — invoices, forms, identity documents entered by hand
5. **Reporting automation** — replacing manual spreadsheet assembly
6. **Support deflection** on the handful of repeated questions

Notice how little of this is exotic. A strategy engagement that returns something dramatically more ambitious is worth questioning, because these are where the measurable returns actually sit.

## When You Do Not Need Consulting

Being direct about this:

- **If you have one obvious painful process**, skip strategy and scope that build.
- **If your processes are undocumented**, document them first. That is not a consulting engagement, it is a fortnight of internal work.
- **If nobody internally can own the outcome**, a strategy document will sit unread.
- **If the real problem is staffing, pricing or your offer**, AI will not fix it and a good consultant will say so.
- **If budget only covers strategy and not implementation**, spend it on implementing one thing instead.

---

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**WhatsApp is the operating system**, not a channel. Any strategy that does not centre it has misunderstood how the business runs.

**Undocumented process knowledge** sitting with long-serving individuals is the norm, and it is the most common blocker to automation. Surfacing it is often the most valuable thing an engagement does.

**Currency exposure on tooling.** SaaS and model usage bill in USD and scale with volume. This materially affects which recommendations are viable at PKR revenue levels.

**Payment access.** Some tools cannot be paid for easily from Pakistan. A recommendation that assumes access to any global tool is incomplete.

**Data reality.** Predictive and analytical recommendations require history most Pakistani SMEs do not have. Honest advisory says so rather than proposing models that cannot be trained.

**Team capability.** Adoption fails more often than technology. Whether someone internally can operate and maintain what gets built should shape every recommendation.

## Conclusion

Buy AI strategy when you have several candidate problems and genuinely do not know which to address first. Skip it when you have one obvious painful process — scope that instead.

Judge the engagement on whether it produces numbers from your business, a sequence, honest constraints, and something another supplier could act on. Everything else is a proposal with extra steps.

## FAQ

**What does an AI strategy engagement deliver?**
A prioritised, costed and sequenced list of processes with build-buy-configure recommendations, honest constraints, and a measurement plan.

**How long should it take?**
Two to four weeks for a mid-sized Pakistani business focused on three to five processes. Longer engagements usually reflect enterprise frameworks rather than your needs.

**Do I need strategy before implementation?**
Only if you have multiple candidate problems and cannot prioritise. With one obvious problem, scope the build.

**How do I know the advice is genuine?**
Ask what they would recommend if you had no implementation budget, and whether the deliverable is usable by another supplier.

**What if we have no data?**
Then predictive recommendations are not viable yet. An honest advisor will say so rather than proposing models that cannot be trained.

**Is our team a factor?**
Substantially. Adoption fails more often than technology, and recommendations should account for who will operate what gets built.

**What usually comes out of these engagements?**
Most often: enquiry response, lead qualification, follow-up, document extraction and reporting. Rarely anything exotic.
