## H1: Website Redesign Services in Pakistan

The most expensive thing that happens to Pakistani business websites is a redesign. Not because rebuilds cost much — because a large share of them destroy search rankings that took years to accumulate, and nobody notices until traffic has already gone.

The cause is almost always the same: URLs changed and nobody mapped redirects. It is entirely preventable, it takes a day of work, and it is routinely left out of scope.

---

## First: Is a Redesign Actually the Answer?

Businesses redesign for the wrong reasons more often than the right ones.

**Bad reasons:**
- "It looks dated." Design preference is not a business case.
- "Our competitor rebuilt theirs."
- "Traffic is down." Diagnose why first — it may be technical, or a ranking loss a rebuild will worsen.
- "We want more leads." A redesign does not fix a weak offer or unanswered enquiries.

**Good reasons:**
- The site cannot be edited without a developer
- It is genuinely slow and the architecture is the constraint
- It does not work properly on phones
- You have outgrown the platform — needing e-commerce, booking, or integrations it cannot support
- Technical problems are capping SEO and cannot be fixed incrementally
- The content no longer describes what the business does

**Frequently the honest answer is a partial rebuild:** keep the structure and URLs, replace the templates and content. Cheaper, faster, and it preserves everything that currently works.

## What to Preserve

Before designing anything, identify what is already working. Most redesigns discard it by accident.

**Pull the data:**
- Which pages receive organic traffic, ranked
- Which pages have external links pointing at them
- Which pages convert
- Which URLs appear in Search Console with impressions
- Which pages are referenced in your Google Business Profile, ads and social bios

**Then decide, page by page:** keep the URL, redirect it, or retire it. Nothing should be deleted without that decision being made deliberately.

The common failure is a new site with a cleaner structure where every URL changed, no redirects were mapped, and six years of accumulated authority evaporated on launch day.

---

## Redirect Mapping: The Non-Negotiable

This must be in the scope, in writing, before work begins.

1. **Export every existing URL** — from your sitemap, Search Console and analytics
2. **Map each to its closest equivalent** on the new site
3. **Implement 301 redirects** — permanent, not 302 temporary
4. **Never redirect everything to the homepage.** Google treats bulk homepage redirects as soft 404s, and you lose the equity anyway
5. **Verify each resolves in one hop** — chained redirects lose value and slow the site
6. **Keep them permanently.** Redirects are not a temporary launch measure

If a developer treats this as optional or as your problem, that is a serious signal about the rest of the engagement.

## The Launch Checklist

Things that go wrong on Pakistani site launches, in rough order of frequency:

- **Staging site left indexable** — the new site competing with itself before launch
- **`noindex` left on the live site** — the entire site disappearing from search
- **Redirects not implemented** or implemented as 302s
- **Analytics and conversion tracking not migrated** — measurement blind from day one
- **Contact forms pointing nowhere**
- **Google Business Profile still linking to an old URL**
- **`robots.txt` blocking resources** the site needs to render
- **Images uploaded at full resolution**, destroying mobile performance
- **Schema markup not carried over**
- **Sitemap not regenerated** or still listing old URLs

Every one of these is checkable in under an hour, and every one has taken down a Pakistani business site.

## Expect a Dip, Not a Collapse

Even a well-executed migration usually sees a short-term fluctuation while search engines recrawl and reassess. That is normal.

**Normal:** a modest dip for two to four weeks, recovering and then improving.

**Not normal:** a sharp sustained drop that does not recover. That indicates a technical problem — usually redirects, indexation, or content that was thinned during the rebuild.

**Watch weekly for eight weeks after launch:** Search Console coverage and impressions, organic sessions, and crawl errors. Catching a problem in week two is straightforward; discovering it in month four is not.

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**Content is usually thinned, not improved.** Redesigns focused on visual simplicity frequently cut the text that was actually ranking. If a page ranks, understand why before shortening it.

**Mobile-first genuinely.** Test on a mid-range Android over mobile data. A redesign that looks better on desktop and performs worse on phones is a downgrade for most Pakistani audiences.

**NAP consistency.** Business name, address and phone on the new site must match your Google Business Profile exactly, in crawlable text rather than inside an image.

**WhatsApp integration.** If click-to-chat existed before, carry it over. If it did not, add it — that is where Pakistani enquiries convert.

**Urdu content.** If the old site had it, plan its structure deliberately rather than dropping it.

**Hosting change.** Redesigns often coincide with a hosting move, which introduces its own risks. Do not change both without confirming DNS, SSL and response time afterwards.

## A Sensible Sequence

1. **Diagnose** — establish whether a redesign is the right answer
2. **Audit** — what currently ranks, converts and has links
3. **Decide URL strategy** — keep or map, page by page
4. **Design and build**, with SEO fundamentals in scope
5. **Map redirects** before launch, not after
6. **Pre-launch checklist**, run fully
7. **Launch**, then monitor weekly for eight weeks
8. **Fix quickly** — early problems are cheap, late ones are not

## FAQ

**Will a redesign hurt my search rankings?**
Only if URLs change without mapped redirects. Done properly, a short fluctuation is normal and recovery follows.

**How long does a redesign take?**
A straightforward site is typically weeks; complex or e-commerce sites take considerably longer. Content readiness is usually the real bottleneck.

**Should I change my URLs?**
Only with a good reason. Every change requires a redirect, and each one loses a little value. Stability is usually worth more than tidiness.

**What if traffic drops after launch?**
Check redirects, indexation and whether content was thinned. A sustained drop indicates a technical problem, not normal fluctuation.

**Can I redesign without losing content?**
Yes, and you should audit first. Pages that rank do so for reasons — understand them before shortening.

**Do I need a full rebuild?**
Often not. New templates on an existing structure preserves rankings and costs less.

**Who should own redirect mapping?**
Whoever builds the site, in writing, before launch. If it is treated as optional, that is a warning about the whole engagement.
