## H1: BITSOL Marketing for E-commerce Businesses

E-commerce in Pakistan has particular economics. Cash on delivery remains common, which means refused orders and returned parcels eat into margins. Customers ask questions on WhatsApp before buying. Ad costs rise during peak seasons. Many stores sell across Instagram, Facebook, marketplaces and their own website at once.

A marketing plan that measures only revenue or return on ad spend can look successful while the business loses money on returns, courier charges and discounts.

BITSOL Marketing works with e-commerce businesses on the full chain — store, traffic, conversion, order confirmation and repeat purchase — measured on what the business actually keeps.

## The Problems We Usually Find

- **High COD refusal and return rates** eroding profit
- **WhatsApp enquiries answered slowly**, losing impulse purchases
- **Ads optimised for purchases without accounting for refused orders**
- **Slow, poorly structured store websites** that lose mobile visitors
- **No product SEO**, leaving the store fully dependent on ads
- **No retention marketing**, despite a large customer list
- **Abandoned carts never followed up**

Each of these costs money independently. Together, they often explain why a store with growing sales has shrinking profit.

## Seasonal Peaks: Planning Instead of Reacting

Pakistani e-commerce revenue concentrates around a handful of periods — Ramadan and Eid, wedding season, 11.11 and year-end sales, back-to-school, and the switch between summer and winter ranges. Ad costs rise at exactly those times, and stores that plan late pay the most for the least.

**How we plan peaks with e-commerce clients:**

- **Six to eight weeks before:** confirm stock depth on the products that will be promoted, prepare product photography and video, and fix any checkout or speed problems while traffic is still normal.
- **Four weeks before:** build retargeting audiences through engagement campaigns, grow the opt-in WhatsApp list, and draft campaign flows for launch, reminders and last-chance messages.
- **Two weeks before:** load campaigns, test WhatsApp order and confirmation flows under realistic volume, and brief customer service staff on handover.
- **During the peak:** monitor delivered-order rates daily, not just orders placed, and pause offers that drive refusals.
- **After the peak:** follow up with first-time buyers to turn them into repeat customers, and analyse which products and audiences were actually profitable.

**The courier constraint matters.** Peak periods strain delivery partners. Promising delivery times couriers cannot meet increases refusals and negative reviews, so campaign messaging should reflect realistic timelines.

Stores that treat peaks as planned projects typically spend less per delivered order than those that increase budgets the week before Eid.
