## H1: WhatsApp Chatbot for Restaurants and Cafes

The commission a delivery platform takes on every order is the most expensive line item most Pakistani restaurants never negotiate. The platform owns the customer, sets the discount, and can change terms whenever it likes.

WhatsApp is the one channel where a restaurant can own the relationship outright. The obstacle has never been the channel — it is that answering fifty simultaneous messages during the dinner rush is impossible for a team that is also cooking and serving.

That is a capacity problem, and it is solvable.

---

## Where the Rush Actually Breaks

Between roughly 8pm and 11pm, three things happen at once: walk-in service peaks, delivery orders peak, and WhatsApp enquiries peak. The messages are almost entirely predictable:

- What is on the menu, and what does it cost?
- Are you open / until when?
- Do you deliver to my area, and what is the charge?
- How long will delivery take?
- Where is my order?
- Can I book a table for tonight?

Every one of these is answerable without judgment, and every one of them costs you an order if it goes unanswered for twenty minutes.

## What to Automate

### Menu and pricing

Sent as a catalogue or document, instantly. Keep it current — an outdated menu produces order disputes, which cost more than they save.

### Delivery area and charges

Ask the area, confirm whether you deliver there, state the charge and the minimum order. This one exchange prevents most abandoned orders.

### Order taking

Item selection, quantity, address, contact number, payment method. For cash on delivery, confirm the order explicitly before dispatch — this filters out prank and impulse orders, which are a genuine cost in Pakistani food delivery.

### Order status

"Where is my order" is high volume and zero judgment. Automated status updates remove it entirely and reduce anxiety-driven cancellations.

### Reservations

Date, time, party size, seating preference, with confirmation and a reminder. Reminders measurably reduce no-shows on weekend bookings.

### Reorder prompts

A previous customer who ordered three weeks ago is the cheapest order you can win. A well-timed, opt-in message performs far better than any advertisement.

## What Should Not Be Automated

- **Complaints about food or service.** These need a person, fast, with authority to fix it.
- **Refunds and compensation.**
- **Large event and catering bookings**, which require negotiation.
- **Allergy and dietary safety questions.** Never let a bot confirm what a dish contains — route to the kitchen.

That last one is not a technicality. A confidently wrong answer about ingredients is a safety and liability issue.

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**Delivery platform dependence is the strategic issue.** Every order taken on WhatsApp is an order without commission and with a customer whose number you keep. Automation makes owning that channel operationally realistic rather than aspirational.

**Cash on delivery dominates**, which makes order confirmation essential. A confirmed order is far less likely to be refused at the door.

**Area-based delivery zones.** Customers describe location by neighbourhood and landmark — Gulberg, Johar Town, DHA Phase 5, "near Chase Up" — rather than by structured address. Build the flow around how people actually give directions, and capture a location pin where possible.

**Ramadan transforms the operation.** Iftar timing creates an extreme, narrow demand spike with hard deadlines, plus sehri service. Pre-orders and scheduled delivery windows become essential, and automated confirmation of delivery time prevents the complaints that otherwise define the month.

**Wedding and event season** drives catering enquiries that should be routed to a human quickly.

**Menu photos matter.** Pakistani customers frequently decide from images. Sending a visual menu outperforms a text list.

## The Reorder Opportunity Most Restaurants Ignore

Once customers order through WhatsApp, you have an opted-in list of people who have already paid you. This is the most valuable marketing asset a restaurant can build, and almost nobody in the sector uses it deliberately.

Used well: occasional, genuinely relevant messages — a new item, a Ramadan pre-order window, a weekend offer to people who have not ordered in a while.

Used badly: daily broadcasts to everyone. That produces blocks, which lower your quality rating, which restricts your ability to message anyone. Restraint here is not politeness, it is protecting a channel you own.

## A Realistic Flow

1. Customer messages, prompted by a table card, receipt, ad or Google Business Profile
2. Instant greeting with options: menu, order, reservation, order status
3. Menu delivered as images or catalogue
4. Delivery area confirmed, charge and minimum stated
5. Order assembled, address and contact captured
6. Explicit confirmation before dispatch
7. Status updates through preparation and delivery
8. Opt-in for future offers

## Conclusion

For a restaurant, WhatsApp automation is about two things: surviving the rush without losing orders, and building a customer list that no platform can take from you or tax.

## FAQ

**Will this replace delivery platforms?**
Not immediately, and probably not entirely. It gives you a commission-free channel alongside them, and a customer list you own.

**Can it take payments?**
It can share payment links and record payment method. Most Pakistani orders remain cash on delivery, which makes explicit confirmation the important step.

**How do we handle wrong or refused orders?**
Confirmation before dispatch reduces them substantially. Those that still occur should reach a person immediately.

**Can it manage reservations?**
Yes — date, time, party size, confirmation and reminders. Reminders reduce weekend no-shows.

**Is it worth it for a small cafe?**
Often yes, if you lose orders during peak hours or already take significant WhatsApp traffic. Start narrow: menu, delivery area and order status.

**What about Ramadan?**
It is the strongest case in the year. Pre-orders, scheduled iftar delivery windows and automated timing confirmations prevent most of the month's complaints.

**Can we message our customer list?**
Only those who opted in, sparingly. Over-messaging produces blocks that reduce your delivery capacity to everyone.
