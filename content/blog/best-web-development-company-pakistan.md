## H1: Best Web Development Company in Pakistan: What to Check Before You Commission a Site

A website is one of the few marketing purchases where you cannot easily judge quality by looking at it. Two sites can appear identical while one loads in one second and the other in nine, one is indexable and the other invisible to Google, one you own and the other you rent.

Everything that separates them is checkable — before you sign, and again before you pay the final invoice. This is the list.

## Check the Portfolio Properly

Do not look at screenshots. Open the live sites and run these checks on each.

1. **Load it on your phone, on mobile data, not office WiFi.** Count the seconds. This is how most Pakistani visitors will experience it.
2. **Search Google for a distinctive sentence from the homepage in quotes.** If nothing returns, the page may not be indexed — a serious sign for a business site.
3. **View the page source and search for the main heading text.** If the visible content is absent from the source, the site renders entirely client-side, which routinely damages search visibility.
4. **Click through to an inner page and check the URL.** Clean and readable, or a string of parameters?
5. **Try the contact form.** A surprising number of delivered sites have forms that go nowhere.
6. **Check whether the site is still live at all.** Portfolios frequently contain dead links.

Two or three failures across a portfolio is a pattern, not bad luck.

## Ownership: The Clause That Matters Most

Before anything technical, settle this.

- **Code.** You own it, delivered in a repository you control. Not "hosted with us."
- **Domain.** Registered in your business name, in an account you can access. Agencies holding client domains is a recurring and avoidable dispute.
- **Hosting.** Your account, agency granted access. If they host it, confirm what happens if you leave.
- **Content and images.** Licensed appropriately, with rights transferring to you.
- **Third-party accounts.** Analytics, Search Console, Tag Manager — created under your email.

A developer who resists any of these is describing a dependency, not a deliverable.

## Technology Choices, Explained Plainly

| Approach | Suits | Consider carefully if |
|---|---|---|
| **WordPress** | Content-heavy sites, blogs, businesses wanting to self-edit | Plugin sprawl and unoptimised themes are the main cause of slow Pakistani sites |
| **Next.js / React** | Custom applications, performance-critical sites, complex functionality | Rendering must be configured for search visibility; ask directly how pages are rendered |
| **Shopify / WooCommerce** | E-commerce with standard requirements | Platform fees versus flexibility |
| **Page builders** | Fast, cheap brochure sites | Export and ownership limits; performance ceiling |

There is no universally correct answer. There is a wrong answer, which is a developer who cannot explain why they chose one for your specific situation beyond "it's what we use."

## What the Scope Should Explicitly Include

Missing items here are where "the website is done" turns into three months of arguments.

- Responsive behaviour on real devices, named
- Page speed target, with the measurement method stated
- SEO fundamentals: crawlable content, meta tags, XML sitemap, clean URLs, schema
- Analytics and conversion tracking, configured and verified
- Contact forms, tested, with the destination confirmed
- WhatsApp integration, since that is where most Pakistani enquiries convert
- Browser support
- Accessibility basics
- A revision allowance, with a number
- Post-launch support period and what it covers
- Training or documentation for updating content
- Backups and security

## Questions That Reveal Competence

1. How will pages be rendered, and will content be in the HTML source?
2. What page speed will you commit to, measured on a mobile connection?
3. Who owns the code, and where will the repository live?
4. What happens if we want to move hosting in a year?
5. What is explicitly out of scope?
6. Who maintains it after launch, and at what cost?
7. If we plan to add e-commerce or a booking system later, does this build allow it?
8. Can I speak to a client whose site you launched over a year ago?

Question eight is the most useful. Anyone can deliver a site; fewer can point to one still performing after a year.

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**Mobile and data reality.** Most visitors arrive on mid-range Android phones over variable connections. Heavy hero images and large JavaScript bundles that feel fine on office broadband produce a genuinely poor experience in practice.

**Hosting location and response time.** Slow server response caps every other performance improvement. Ask where the site will be hosted and what time-to-first-byte to expect for Pakistani visitors.

**Payment gateway integration.** For e-commerce, confirm which local gateways are supported and who handles the integration and its approval process. This frequently takes longer than the build.

**Cash on delivery.** Still central to Pakistani e-commerce. Order confirmation, address verification and delivery coordination need designing, not bolting on.

**Bilingual content.** If you plan Urdu content, raise it before the build. Retrofitting language structure is more expensive than planning it.

**Redesign risk.** Rebuilding an existing site without mapping redirects is the most common way Pakistani businesses lose their search rankings overnight. Confirm redirect mapping is in scope and in writing.

## Warning Signs

- A quote given before anyone asked what the site must achieve
- No mention of speed, indexing or tracking anywhere in the proposal
- Templates presented as custom design
- Payment structured entirely up front
- No written scope, only a price
- Reluctance to hand over repository access
- A portfolio of sites that are no longer live

## FAQ

**How much does a website cost in Pakistan?**
It depends on custom design versus template, functionality, integrations and content. Get the scope itemised so proposals are comparable.

**How long does a business website take?**
A straightforward brochure site is typically weeks; custom applications and e-commerce with integrations take considerably longer. Content readiness is usually the real bottleneck.

**Should I choose WordPress or a custom build?**
WordPress suits content-led sites you want to edit yourself. Custom builds suit performance-critical sites and genuine application functionality.

**Will a new website improve my Google rankings?**
Only if the current site's problems were technical. A rebuild without redirect mapping frequently makes rankings worse.

**Who should own the domain and hosting?**
You, in every case. Grant access to your developer rather than the reverse.

**What happens after launch?**
Agree the support period, what it covers, and the maintenance cost before signing. Sites need updates, backups and security attention.

**Can I update the site myself?**
Ask for it explicitly. It is a design decision, and it is far cheaper to build in than to add later.
