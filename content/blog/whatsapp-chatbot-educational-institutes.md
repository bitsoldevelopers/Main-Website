## H1: WhatsApp Chatbot for Educational Institutes

Admission season does not arrive gradually. Enquiry volume for a Pakistani academy or school can multiply several times over within a fortnight, land almost entirely on WhatsApp, and consist overwhelmingly of the same six questions.

Meanwhile the staff answering those messages are the same people processing applications, conducting interviews and handling walk-ins. Something gives, and what usually gives is response time — during the exact weeks when parents are comparing three institutes and choosing the one that answered.

---

## The Six Questions That Are Most of Your Volume

Before designing anything, look at last season's messages. In almost every Pakistani institute the distribution is similar:

1. **What are the fees?** — asked more than anything else, often first
2. **What is the admission criteria / merit?**
3. **When does the session start, and is admission still open?**
4. **What is the timing / schedule?**
5. **Where exactly are you located?**
6. **Is there a discount, scholarship or instalment plan?**

None of these need a human. All of them need an immediate answer, because a parent asking about fees at 9pm is comparing you against two other institutes that same evening.

---

## What the Bot Should Handle

**Fee structure** — by programme, class or course, with instalment options stated plainly. Institutes often hesitate to publish fees; withholding them mostly filters out serious parents rather than protecting margin.

**Eligibility and merit criteria** — required marks, prior qualification, entry test details.

**Session dates and deadlines** — with the current cycle's actual dates, not last year's.

**Programme details** — subjects, duration, faculty, class size.

**Location and timings** — with a map link.

**Prospectus or fee-structure document** — sent directly in chat.

**Qualification** — which programme, which class, student's current level, city or area. This turns a raw enquiry into something the admissions team can act on.

**Callback booking** — for parents who want to speak to someone, scheduled rather than promised vaguely.

---

## What Must Reach a Human

- Scholarship and fee-concession negotiation
- Complaints about staff or existing students
- Anything about a currently enrolled student's performance or conduct
- Disciplinary matters
- Refund requests

Handing these to automation damages trust with exactly the families you most want to keep.

## The Seasonality Case

Most of the year, an institute's enquiry volume is manageable by hand. That is why many owners conclude they do not need automation — and then lose applications for six weeks every year.

The economics only make sense when measured across the peak. Before your next cycle, record three numbers during the busiest fortnight:

- Total enquiries received
- How many received a reply within an hour
- How many received no reply at all

The third number is usually the one that changes minds. An institute that misses 40 enquiries in a season, at typical Pakistani annual fee levels, has lost far more than any automation costs.

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**Parents message, students call.** The person enquiring is frequently a parent, often writing in Roman Urdu, sometimes forwarding the question from a relative. The bot must handle mixed-language input and a formal, respectful tone.

**Evening and Sunday traffic is heavy.** Working parents research after hours. This is precisely when admissions offices are closed, and precisely where automation earns its cost.

**Fee sensitivity is high and comparison is active.** Parents genuinely shortlist on cost. Answering the fee question instantly and clearly outperforms deflecting it.

**Voice notes are common** from parents less comfortable typing. Decide whether to transcribe or route them to a person — ignoring them loses real families.

**Document requests.** Prospectus PDFs, fee structures, timetables and admission forms are all routinely requested. Sending them in chat removes a step that otherwise requires a visit.

**Result and board announcement spikes.** Matric and intermediate result days produce sharp, predictable enquiry surges. Plan capacity around the board calendar.

**Transport and hostel questions** matter more than most institutes anticipate, especially for students coming from surrounding towns.

## A Sensible Flow

1. Parent messages — from a Facebook ad, Google Business Profile, the website or a flyer
2. Instant greeting that states you are an automated assistant and a person is available
3. Ask what they need: admission info, fees, programmes, or something else
4. Answer directly from your real, current information
5. Qualify: which programme, which class, student's current level, area
6. Offer the prospectus and a callback slot
7. Route to admissions with the summary attached
8. Follow up after two days if the family went quiet

Step eight is where most institutes leave money. A parent who enquired and did not return has usually not decided — they have been distracted.

## What Changes

Institutes that deploy this typically see the same pattern: the share of enquiries answered rises sharply, first-response time collapses from hours to seconds, admissions staff spend their time on interviews rather than repeating the fee structure, and the follow-up sequence recovers families who would otherwise have quietly disappeared.

The metric worth tracking is enquiries-to-applications, measured against last season with the same advertising spend.

## FAQ

**Will parents accept talking to a bot?**
Yes, when the answer is immediate and useful and a person is clearly reachable. Parents object to waiting, not to being answered quickly.

**Should we publish our fee structure?**
Generally yes. Withholding it mostly deters serious enquirers, who will simply ask a competitor who answers.

**Can it handle Urdu and Roman Urdu?**
It should, and this must be tested on real parent messages rather than clean English scripts.

**When should we build it?**
Well before admission season. Building during the peak means testing on live traffic at the worst possible time.

**Can it send the prospectus?**
Yes — documents can be delivered directly in chat, which frequently removes the need for a first visit.

**What should never be automated?**
Fee concessions, complaints, and anything concerning an enrolled student.

**How do we measure whether it worked?**
Compare enquiries answered, first-response time, and enquiries-to-applications against the same season last year at comparable ad spend.
