## H1: SEO Strategy for New Websites

A new website starts with no history, no links and no reputation with search engines. That does not make ranking impossible — but it does mean the strategy that works for an established site will usually fail for a new one.

The most common mistake is targeting the same competitive keywords as long-established competitors on day one, publishing content for months, and concluding that SEO does not work. It works; the targets were wrong.

This guide sets out what to do before launch and in the first six months.

---

## Before Launch: Get the Foundations Right

Fixing SEO problems after launch is always harder than avoiding them.

### Technical essentials

- **Indexable pages** — no leftover "noindex" tags or blocked robots.txt from development
- **HTTPS** across the site
- **Clean URL structure** — short, readable, logical folders
- **Mobile-first design** that loads quickly on average connections
- **XML sitemap** submitted to Google Search Console
- **Canonical tags** preventing duplicate versions
- **Server-rendered content** — key content should not depend on JavaScript to appear
- **Structured data** for the organisation, local business, articles or products as relevant

### Tracking

- Google Search Console verified
- Analytics installed with conversion events — forms, calls, WhatsApp clicks
- Baseline recorded from day one

### Site architecture

Plan pages around what customers search, not around your internal organisation. One page per core service or product, one page per location genuinely served, and a logical place for supporting content.

If replacing an old website, **map old URLs to new ones with redirects** before launch. Lost redirects are one of the most damaging and avoidable SEO failures.

## Month 1: Choose Winnable Keywords

New sites should target queries where they can realistically compete.

**Prioritise:**
- **Long-tail queries** — specific, lower volume, clearer intent
- **Local queries** — service plus city or area
- **Niche and specialist queries** where competitors are generic
- **Question queries** that existing results answer poorly
- **Brand and product-specific queries** you should own

**Deprioritise for now:**
- Broad head terms dominated by established sites
- Queries where results are all large publishers, marketplaces or directories

**How to judge difficulty without tools:** search the query. If results are dominated by major national brands, directories and long-established sites with extensive content, it is not a first-six-months target. If results include small businesses, thin pages or forum threads, it is winnable.

## Month 1–2: Build Core Pages Properly

Before blogging, make the pages that make money excellent.

**Each core service or product page should:**
- Target one primary query clearly
- Answer what buyers need to know — scope, process, pricing guidance, timelines
- Include genuine proof — examples, case studies, reviews
- Address common objections and questions
- Link logically to related pages
- Have a clear call to action

A handful of strong commercial pages outperforms dozens of thin ones.

---

## Month 2–4: Build Topical Focus

Search engines increasingly reward sites that demonstrate depth on a subject.

**Choose one or two topic clusters** closely connected to what you sell. For each:
- One comprehensive pillar page
- Supporting articles answering specific questions
- Internal links between them and up to the relevant service page

A new site covering one topic thoroughly tends to outrank a new site covering twenty topics shallowly. Our guide to [entity SEO and topical authority](/blog/entity-seo-topical-authority) explains why.

**Content quality standards:**
- Written from genuine expertise
- Specific details, examples and data where available
- Clear structure that answers the query quickly
- Named authorship and real business information

## Local Businesses: Start With Google Business Profile

For businesses serving a local area, Google Business Profile often produces results before organic rankings do.

- Correct categories, address or service area, hours and contact details
- Genuine photographs
- Early review generation from real customers
- Consistent name, address and phone across directories

## Month 3–6: Earn First Links and Mentions

New sites need signals that others consider them legitimate.

**Realistic early link sources:**
- Relevant business directories and industry associations
- Supplier, partner and client websites
- Local business and chamber listings
- Guest contributions to relevant industry publications
- Genuinely useful resources others want to reference
- Founder interviews and podcast appearances
- Digital PR around real data or stories

**Avoid:** bought link packages, private blog networks and mass directory submissions. Early spam signals can hold a new site back for a long time.

## What to Expect, Month by Month

| Period | Realistic expectation |
|---|---|
| Month 1 | Pages indexed; impressions begin appearing in Search Console |
| Months 2–3 | Rankings for long-tail and branded queries; Maps visibility for local businesses |
| Months 3–6 | Growing impressions; first consistent organic enquiries on niche queries |
| Months 6–12 | Movement on moderately competitive terms as content and links accumulate |

Competitive markets take longer. Our guide to [how long SEO takes](/blog/how-long-does-seo-take) explains the variables.

## Mistakes New Sites Make

- **Launching with noindex still in place**
- **No redirects from an old site**
- **Targeting only broad, competitive keywords**
- **Publishing many thin blog posts instead of strong core pages**
- **Covering too many unrelated topics**
- **Buying links for quick results**
- **Not tracking conversions from the start**
- **Changing URLs repeatedly in the first months**

## Conclusion

SEO for a new website is a sequencing problem. Build clean technical foundations before launch, target queries a new site can win, make core pages excellent, build depth in a focused topic, and earn legitimate early links.

New sites that follow that order build momentum. New sites that chase competitive keywords from day one usually stall.

## FAQ

**How long does it take a new website to rank?**
Long-tail and branded queries can rank within the first few months. Competitive terms usually take six to twelve months or longer.

**Should a new website start a blog immediately?**
Build strong core service or product pages first, then add focused supporting content.

**Can a new website rank for competitive keywords?**
Eventually, but targeting winnable long-tail and local queries first builds the authority needed.

**Is buying links a good way to speed up a new site?**
No. Low-quality bought links carry significant risk and can hold a new site back.

**What should be done before a website launches?**
Check indexability, set up HTTPS, sitemap, Search Console, analytics, structured data, and redirects from any old site.

**Does domain age matter?**
Age itself matters less than accumulated content quality, relevance and legitimate links — which naturally take time.
