## H1: Best Social Media Marketing Agency in Pakistan: Judging One by Its Output

Social media is the easiest marketing service to buy and the hardest to hold anyone accountable for. Followers grow, posts go out, engagement happens — and nobody can say whether any of it produced a customer.

That accountability gap is where most social budgets in Pakistan quietly disappear. The fix is not finding a better agency; it is changing what you ask them to be measured on before you sign.

---

## First, Decide What You Are Buying It For

These are different jobs and they need different agencies.

**Demand generation.** Reaching people who do not yet know they need you. Judged on qualified enquiries and cost per enquiry.

**Consideration and trust.** People already researching you check your profile before buying. A dead or chaotic page loses sales that other channels earned. Judged on whether the page answers a prospect's questions.

**Community and retention.** Existing customers, repeat purchase, referral. Judged on repeat business and response quality.

**Recruitment and employer brand.** Increasingly relevant for Pakistani tech and services firms.

Most businesses need the second one and buy the first. Be explicit about which, because an agency optimising for reach will not build the page that closes a hesitant buyer.

## How to Judge a Content Sample in Ten Minutes

Ask any shortlisted agency for three months of a current client's output, anonymised. Then check:

- **Does anything say what the business actually sells?** A great deal of Pakistani social output is generic motivational content with a logo attached. It performs adequately and sells nothing.
- **Is there a reason to act?** Not every post, but some proportion should give a viewer a next step.
- **Does it look like the brand, or like a template?** Swap the logo — if it could belong to any business, it does not belong to yours.
- **Is there variety in format?** Static, carousel, video, story, and — for Pakistani audiences — content that works with sound off.
- **Are the captions written by someone who understands the business?** This is usually obvious within three posts.
- **Do comments and DMs get answered?** Check the client's actual page. Unanswered comments on a managed page tell you where the money went.

## Platform Fit in Pakistan

| Platform | Works well for | Reality check |
|---|---|---|
| **Facebook** | Broad local reach, community groups, older demographics, marketplace-style selling | Still the widest reach in most Pakistani cities |
| **Instagram** | Visual categories — food, fashion, salons, interiors, fitness, real estate | Strong in urban centres; weaker reach outside them |
| **TikTok** | Fast organic reach, younger audiences, entertainment-led categories | Reach is achievable without spend, but the content style is specific |
| **LinkedIn** | B2B, professional services, software, recruitment | Small audience, high value per person |
| **YouTube** | Depth, tutorials, high-consideration purchases | Highest production cost, longest compounding return |
| **WhatsApp** | Where the conversation actually converts | Not a broadcast channel; treat it as the conversion layer |

An agency proposing all six for an SME is not planning, it is quoting.

## What the Engagement Should Include

- A content calendar agreed in advance, not posted and explained afterwards
- Production: design, copy, and video editing, with named responsibility
- Publishing and scheduling
- **Community management** — comments, DMs and reviews answered within a stated time
- Paid amplification, if included, with the ad spend separated from the management fee
- Monthly reporting on enquiries generated, not just reach and engagement

Community management is the item most often quietly dropped. It is also where enquiries arrive. Confirm it is in scope and confirm the response-time commitment.

---

## Pricing Structures

**Monthly retainer by content volume.** Most common. Priced on posts, stories, videos and platforms per month. Ask exactly how many of each, and who produces the video.

**Retainer plus ad management.** Management fee separate from ad spend. Never accept these blended — you cannot evaluate either.

**Project-based campaigns.** Launches, Ramadan campaigns, seasonal pushes. Clear scope, clear end.

The variable that moves price most is video production. Static design is comparatively cheap; well-edited short-form video is not, and it is what actually performs.

## Red Flags

- **Follower growth as the headline metric.** Followers can be bought, and bought followers destroy your reach.
- **No question about what a customer is worth to you.**
- **Pages managed under the agency's own Business Manager.** Your Meta assets must sit in your Business Manager with the agency granted access.
- **Content approved in bulk with no strategy document.**
- **No community management in scope.**
- **Identical content plans for businesses in different cities and categories.**

## Pakistan-Specific Considerations

**Sound-off viewing and data cost.** Many viewers watch without sound and on limited data. Captions and early visual hooks matter more here than in markets with cheap unlimited data.

**Language mix.** Roman Urdu, Urdu and English all perform, but the right mix depends on category and city. An agency that only writes formal English is guessing.

**Seasonality is severe.** Ramadan, Eid, wedding season and admission cycles shift both demand and attention patterns. Ask to see how the calendar accounts for them.

**Comment volume is the lead source.** In many Pakistani categories, buyers comment "price?" rather than clicking through. If nobody answers those within minutes, the campaign generated cost and not revenue.

**Creator collaboration is often cheaper than ads.** Micro-creators in specific cities frequently outperform paid reach for local businesses. Ask whether the agency has actually run these.

## FAQ

**How much does social media management cost in Pakistan?**
It depends on content volume, video production and how many platforms are covered. Ask for the monthly deliverables itemised by format so proposals compare properly.

**How many posts per month do I need?**
Consistency matters more than volume. A smaller number of well-made posts with real community management beats daily filler.

**Should I be on TikTok?**
If your audience is there and you can produce content in the platform's style, it offers reach without spend. If you cannot commit to the format, the effort is better spent elsewhere.

**Do followers matter?**
Only as a by-product. Reach, enquiries and conversion matter. Purchased followers actively damage reach.

**Should the agency run my ads too?**
Often it is more efficient, since creative and targeting are related. Keep the management fee and the ad spend separately itemised.

**Who should own the Business Manager?**
You. Always. The agency should be granted access to assets you own.
