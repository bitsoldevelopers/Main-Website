## H1: How WhatsApp Chatbots Generate Leads for Businesses

Most Pakistani businesses do not have a lead generation problem on WhatsApp. They have a lead capture problem.

The enquiries arrive — from ads, Instagram, Google Maps, referrals and packaging — but many are never turned into leads. Messages go unanswered after hours. Replies come too late. Customer details are never recorded. Undecided customers are never contacted again.

A WhatsApp chatbot generates more leads mainly by capturing demand that already exists. This article explains how, step by step, using ConversiQ — BITSOL Marketing's done-for-you WhatsApp AI chatbot — as the working example.

---

## Where WhatsApp Leads Are Lost

### After-hours enquiries

Customers message when they are free — evenings, late nights, Sundays. If the business replies the next morning, the customer has often already contacted, and sometimes bought from, someone else.

### Slow replies during working hours

Even in office hours, staff are busy with customers in front of them, calls and other tasks. A message that waits an hour competes with every other business the customer contacted.

### No systematic capture

When a conversation ends without a sale, the customer's name, need and budget usually remain buried in a chat thread. There is no lead record, so there is nothing to follow up.

### No follow-up

Customers who said "let me think" or "I'll check with my family" are a large share of real buyers. Without reminders, they drift away.

### Inconsistent answers

Different staff give different prices or information, and some customers lose confidence.

Every one of these losses happens before a lead ever appears in a report — which is why most businesses underestimate them.

## The Lead Capture System, Step by Step

### Step 1: Instant engagement

The chatbot replies within seconds to every message, at any hour. It greets the customer, acknowledges the enquiry and answers the first question — price, availability, location, timings — from approved business information.

ConversiQ handles Urdu, English and Roman Urdu, matching how Pakistani customers actually write.

### Step 2: Qualification

Once the customer's first question is answered, the chatbot asks a small number of qualifying questions relevant to the business:

- **Real estate:** buyer or investor, budget, plot or unit size, timeline
- **Clinics:** service needed, preferred doctor, preferred day
- **Education:** class or programme, campus, start date
- **B2B services:** company type, requirement, timeline
- **E-commerce:** product interest, size, city

Good qualification feels like helpful service, not an interrogation. Three to five questions is usually enough.

### Step 3: Automatic lead logging

Every conversation creates a lead record with name, number, source, need and qualification answers — in a CRM, Google Sheet or dashboard. Leads stop living only in chat history.

### Step 4: Routing and handover

Qualified, high-intent leads are routed immediately to the right salesperson with the conversation attached. Complex questions, complaints and high-value enquiries are handed to staff. Lower-intent leads receive useful information and enter follow-up.

### Step 5: Catalogue and information delivery

The chatbot shares brochures, price lists, product catalogues, payment plans or location pins at the moment the customer asks — without staff searching for files.

### Step 6: Re-engagement

Customers who did not convert receive timely follow-up: a reminder, an answer to a likely objection, an update or an offer. Follow-up respects opt-in rules and stops when a customer asks it to.

## Designing Qualification That Works

- **Ask only what sales needs** to prioritise the lead
- **Answer the customer's question first**, then qualify
- **Use buttons** where answers are predictable
- **Allow free text** for needs that vary
- **Never block access to a person**
- **Test questions in Urdu and Roman Urdu** with real customer phrasing
