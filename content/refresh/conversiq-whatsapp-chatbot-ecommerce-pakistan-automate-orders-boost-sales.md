## H1: WhatsApp Chatbot for E-commerce and Online Stores in Pakistan

For Pakistani online stores, WhatsApp is often where the real sale happens. Customers find a product on Instagram, Facebook, TikTok or a website — and then message to ask: Is it available? What sizes? How much is delivery? Can I pay cash on delivery? When will it arrive?

Every one of those questions is a potential order. Answered slowly, or not at all, it becomes a sale for another store.

A WhatsApp chatbot automates that journey from the first question to post-delivery follow-up. This guide walks through each stage, then covers set-up and measurement.

---

## The Complete E-commerce Journey on WhatsApp

### Stage 1: Product discovery

Customers arrive from ads, social posts, influencer content, website buttons or packaging inserts. Click-to-WhatsApp ads and pre-filled messages tell the chatbot which product or campaign the customer came from, so the first reply is relevant.

### Stage 2: Product questions and catalogue browsing

The chatbot answers:
- Price and current offers
- Available sizes, colours and variants
- Stock availability
- Fabric, materials, dimensions and specifications
- Delivery charges and delivery times by city
- Payment options
- Return and exchange policy

It can share product images, catalogue links or collections, and suggest alternatives when an item is out of stock.

### Stage 3: Order collection

Once a customer wants to buy, the chatbot collects:
- Product, variant and quantity
- Name
- Delivery address and city
- Phone number
- Payment method

It then summarises the order for the customer to confirm, reducing address and variant errors. Orders are sent to the store's system, spreadsheet or staff for processing.

### Stage 4: COD confirmation

Cash on delivery remains common in Pakistan, and refused deliveries cost stores courier charges, handling time and stock held in transit.

The chatbot sends a confirmation message before dispatch asking the customer to confirm the order and address. Unconfirmed orders can be held, followed up by staff or cancelled according to store policy. This filters out many orders placed without genuine intent before they incur costs.

### Stage 5: Delivery updates

Automated messages for order dispatched, tracking number, out for delivery and delivered — reducing "where is my order?" messages.

### Stage 6: Returns and exchanges

The chatbot explains the policy, collects the order number and reason, requests photos where needed and routes the request to staff for approval.

### Stage 7: Post-delivery follow-up

- Satisfaction check
- Review request
- Care instructions or usage tips
- Recommendations for related products
- Opt-in for new arrivals and offers

## For Online Stores Without a Website

Many Pakistani sellers operate entirely through Instagram, Facebook or TikTok, with WhatsApp as their checkout. For these stores, a chatbot effectively becomes the store's sales assistant and order system.

**What works especially well:**
- **Catalogue sharing inside WhatsApp** in place of a website product page
- **Structured order forms in chat**, so orders are not scattered through DMs and screenshots
- **Order sheets updated automatically**, replacing manual copying
- **COD confirmation** before booking couriers
- **Broadcast campaigns to opted-in customers** for new drops and restocks

**What to watch:**
- Keep stock information current — overselling damages trust quickly
- Use the official WhatsApp Business Platform once volume grows, rather than personal or unofficial tools
- Build a customer list you own, independent of any social platform

## Why Pakistan-Specific Design Matters

**Roman Urdu.** Customers write "size L available hai?" or "COD ho jayega?". The chatbot needs to understand real phrasing, not just standard English.

**COD-first flows.** Confirmation and address accuracy should be built into ordering, not added later.

**City-based delivery information.** Charges and delivery times vary by city and courier.

**Low-bandwidth consideration.** Lightweight images and concise messages load better on mobile data.

**Seasonal peaks.** Eid, 11.11 and wedding season create message spikes staff cannot handle manually.

## Set-Up Checklist

1. **Product data** — prices, variants, stock source, images
2. **Delivery information** — charges, cities, timelines, couriers
3. **Policies** — returns, exchanges, payment
4. **Order destination** — store platform, Google Sheet or order management tool
5. **COD confirmation rules** — timing, reminders, what happens to unconfirmed orders
6. **Handover rules** — complaints, custom requests, bulk orders
7. **Tracking integration** — courier updates where available
8. **Opt-in collection** for campaigns
9. **Testing** — real customer phrasing in Urdu, Roman Urdu and English
